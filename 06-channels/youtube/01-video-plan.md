# Video Plan — 12-Week Parent Pivot

## SCOPE FENCE

Organic slate. No CPTD gate on any slot — organic has no spend denominator. No frequency-fatigue rule; evergreen video appreciates. [[forbidden-patterns]] and [[brand-book-v2]] apply in full.

---

## The portfolio principle

Reach, engagement, subscribers and conversion cannot be served by one content type. A channel that tries produces 100K subscribers and zero trials — which is roughly the position we are recovering from.

So the plan is a **portfolio with weights**, and the weights *are* the strategy. Every slot gets one primary KPI and one metric it is explicitly allowed to lose.

| Format | Primary KPI | Serves | Allowed to lose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shorts | Views · subs/1000 views | Reach, subscribers | Conversion, watch time |
| Grade / topic explainer | Search impressions · AVD | Evergreen reach, trust | Immediate conversion |
| Parent-decision long-form | LP click-through · trial signups | **Conversion** | Views, ER |
| Session glimpse | LP click-through | **Conversion** | Views |
| Parent testimonial | Trial signups · pool growth | **Conversion** | Reach |
| Hub film (3–6 min) | Completion rate · returning viewers | Engagement, brand | Conversion, views |
| Hero doc | Subscribers · brand lift | Subscribers, halo | Everything short-term |

---

## Cadence — pick one and hold it

Consistency beats volume on YouTube. A cadence that slips is worse than a slower cadence that holds, because the algorithm learns an upload rhythm and the audience learns an expectation.

### Full cadence — 17 assets / month

- 12 Shorts (3/week)
- 4 long-form (1/week, rotating the four formats)
- 1 hub film

### Minimum viable cadence — 9 assets / month *(recommend starting here)*

- 6 Shorts (biweekly pairs)
- 2 long-form (1 conversion-format + 1 explainer)
- 1 hub film

Start at minimum viable, prove the pipeline holds for a month, then step up. A slate that collapses in week five costs more than it ever produced — and during a pivot, an upload gap reads to the algorithm as a dead channel exactly when we need it re-learning who we're for.

### Weights, whichever cadence

Roughly **35% conversion formats · 35% reach formats · 20% trust formats · 10% brand.** The conversion share is deliberately high for the first quarter; this is a channel that has never had any.

### Shorts, all 12 weeks

Three sources, roughly equal thirds:

1. **Cut-downs** from that week's long-form — the single strongest 30 seconds, vertical, recut.
2. **Coach micro-moments** — one question, one wait, one child working it out. No explanation, no CTA.
3. **Parent one-liners** — a single observed-behaviour line from a testimonial. *"He explained it back to me."*

Shorts serve reach and subscribers, and they pull into long-form via the end-screen and pinned comment. They are **not** judged on conversion, and a Short that converts is a happy accident, not a target.

## Upload discipline

| | |
|---|---|
| Timing | US morning — target 7–9am ET. The first hours' audience defines the video's initial signal, and we want that audience to be American parents |
| Days | Hold a fixed pattern. Tue / Thu for long-form, weekend for Shorts |
| Shorts → long-form | Every Short ends on the long-form it was cut from. This is the cheapest funnel on the platform |
| Playlist on publish | Every upload enters a playlist at publish, at a chosen position. Never "add later" — the route is part of the asset |
| Premiere | Not for a pivot. Premieres concentrate the *existing* audience, which is the audience we are trying to move away from |

## Paid-seeding intent

Marquee specifies intent; the paid surfaces own the buy.

| | |
|---|---|
| Window | First 48 hours after publish, every upload from week 5 |
| Audience | US · parents of school-age children · the standard US parent cells, NRI-weighted per the campaign mix |
| Objective | Video views / Demand Gen — **audience acquisition, not conversion** |
| Success | % of first-week views that are US and parent-age. Not CPV, not CPTD |
| Why | The organic algorithm's first read on a new video comes from whoever watches first. Seeding buys that first read. It is the difference between a 90-day pivot and an 18-month one |

**Do not judge seeding spend on a CPTD basis.** It will look terrible and it is not what it is for. This needs to be said to whoever reviews the media report, in advance.

## Kill and escalate criteria

| Signal | At | Action |
|---|---|---|
| India >35% of views on a new upload | 72h | Re-package: new thumbnail, new title. Not new content |
| 18–24 >30% of views | 72h | Same — the packaging read as student or enthusiast |
| Suggested-from-own-old-videos is the #1 traffic source | Weekly | Legacy library is still defining the channel. Fence harder |
| A format misses its primary KPI 3 uploads running | Rolling | Re-brief the format, or cut the slot and reallocate the weight |
| % US views flat after 8 weeks *with* seeding running | Week 8 | Escalate. The problem is upstream of packaging — likely channel fragmentation, decision D1 |

Note what is **not** on this list: engagement rate, views, subscriber count. Those are expected to fall. See [[00-channel-revamp-brief]] §4.
