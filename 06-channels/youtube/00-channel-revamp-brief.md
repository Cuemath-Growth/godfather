# YouTube Channel Revamp Brief — @CuemathStudio

## SCOPE FENCE

Organic owned-channel work. The paid-performance rulebook does **not** apply here — no CPTD gates, no RSA character limits, no [[rsa-preflight-audit]], no Meta text-on-image rule, no frequency-based fatigue. Full fence in [[01-agents/07-marquee|Marquee]].

What **does** apply, in full: [[forbidden-patterns]] · [[brand-book-v2]] · market verified-facts tables for anything numeric.

---

## 1. The brief, as given

> The audience is not parents in the US but mainly math enthusiasts from all over, majorly India. While the ER is good, conversions will never happen as the audience is not of high intent. This is the new objective going forwards — revamping the existing page, redoing the thumbnails with an image of a child / parent and coach, keeping in mind the new brand guidelines.

Accepted as the objective. Three things get added to it below, because the brief as written will not get there on its own.

---

## 2. Diagnosis

### The channel is not broken. It is aimed.

The current channel presents as *"Math is simple, fun, beautiful and easy to master"* — edutainment for people who already like math. That positioning is doing exactly what it says, and it is recruiting exactly who it promises: enthusiasts.

Note that this one line breaks three [[brand-book-v2]] rules on its own — **"easy"** (banned: we make math *meaningful*, not easy), **"fun"** (K–2 only, never HS), and **"simple"** as a claim about the subject rather than about the teaching. The positioning line is the first thing to go.

### The mechanism was the thumbnails

Equation-forward, puzzle-forward, symbol-wall thumbnails select for people who enjoy looking at equations. They are a filter, and the filter has been running for years. Redoing the thumbnails is not cosmetic — **it is the entire targeting instrument on organic YouTube.** Naina's instinct here is correct and is the highest-leverage single move available.

### Why good ER is the trap

ER is the metric that let this run. Enthusiasts are *more* engaged than parents by nature — they comment, argue about method, rewatch, share into math communities. A parent watches once, quietly, and leaves to go look at pricing. **High ER with zero conversion is the signature of an audience that enjoys the content and will never buy the product.**

ER is hereby demoted. It is not a channel health metric for a parent-acquisition channel.

### The three things the brief is missing

**(a) The audience graph is sticky, and it fights the pivot.**
YouTube tests every upload against the existing viewer base first — subscribers, and the suggested-video sidebar of prior uploads. That base is India-enthusiast. When a parent-facing video goes out, *they* supply the first-24-hour click-through and watch-time signal. They will skip it. The algorithm reads that as "weak video," not "right video, wrong room," and suppresses it. New packaging alone therefore under-performs for the first several weeks *even when the packaging is correct.*

**(b) You cannot target geography organically. You have to buy the new audience graph.**
There is no US-parent dropdown on an upload. The organic levers are indirect: American spelling and grade vocabulary, on-screen curriculum cues (Common Core, AMC 8, MATHCOUNTS, SAT/AP), casting, upload timing to US mornings, and Shorts pulling into long-form. All real, all slow. The fast lever is **paid seeding** — pushing each new upload to US parent audiences in its first 48 hours via Video Views / Demand Gen, so the algorithm's *first* signal about that video comes from the audience we want. This is the single difference between a 90-day pivot and an 18-month one. Budget it as audience acquisition, not as conversion media.

**(c) There is more than one Cuemath channel.**
`@CuemathStudio`, `@cuemath_academy`, and at least one further channel (`Cuemath: Math Games & Classes`) are all live. Before rebuilding a shelf, we need to know which channel is the destination and what happens to the others — otherwise we split the audience graph three ways and none of them compounds. This is decision D1 in §7.

### What a rename does not fix

Changing the handle, banner, or description does not touch the audience graph. Nothing resets it except new packaging plus new viewer behaviour. If a rebrand gets proposed as the fix, this is the answer.

---

## 3. The new objective

**Turn the channel from a reach asset into a parent-acquisition asset — and into the first-party remarketing pool that paid conversion campaigns run off.**

That second half is the part that makes the conversion goal actually achievable. YouTube channel engagers are a targetable audience segment. A channel full of US parents is a compounding remarketing pool worth real money. A channel full of India enthusiasts is a pool worth nothing — which is precisely why conversions "will never happen" today. Fix the audience and conversion stops being a content problem.

### The audience, named

| | |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | US parents of grades 3–10. NRI-leaning but not NRI-exclusive |
| **Secondary** | US parents of grades 9–12 (SAT / AP / competition track) |
| **Deliberately repelled** | Math enthusiasts · competitive-puzzle audiences · students seeking homework answers · India-based general audience |

That third row is not collateral damage. It is the plan. Every packaging decision must be able to name who it turns away.

## 4. The 90-day re-aim protocol

### Phase 0 — Baseline (week 0, before any upload)

Freeze new enthusiast content. Pull from YouTube Studio and record here:

- Geography: % views US / India / other
- Age & gender: % 35–54 (parent proxy) vs % 18–24 (enthusiast proxy)
- Traffic sources: browse / suggested / search / external, ranked
- New vs returning viewers
- Subs per 1000 views
- Top 20 by views **and** separately top 20 by watch time — the two lists diverging tells you which content the algorithm is actually using to define the channel

No plan gets judged without this baseline. Without it, week 12 has nothing to compare to.

### Phase 1 — Re-package (weeks 1–4)

1. **Fence the legacy library.** Unlist or de-feature enthusiast content. Never delete — it is reversible and some of it may be re-aimable. Keep the ~10 strongest in one unfeatured playlist, off the funnel path.
2. **Re-package, don't just re-publish.** YouTube re-tests packaging on old videos. Any existing video with parent-relevant substance gets a new title + new thumbnail rather than being buried. This is free inventory and the fastest visible movement in the whole plan.
3. **Rewrite the shelf** — trailer, About, channel sections, playlists. Per [[03-playlist-architecture]].
4. **Ship net-new:** 4 parent-facing long-form + 12 Shorts. Per [[01-video-plan]].
5. **All new thumbnails on the new system.** Per [[02-thumbnail-system]].

### Phase 2 — Seed (weeks 5–8)

Every new upload gets paid-seeded to US parent audiences within 48 hours of publish. This is the phase that actually moves the audience graph. Continue the Phase 1 cadence.

Hand the buy to the paid surfaces — Marquee specifies seeding intent (audience, window, budget shape), not the media plan.

### Phase 3 — Convert (weeks 9–12)

The remarketing pool is now majority US-parent. Layer Demand Gen conversion campaigns off channel-engager audiences. Judge conversion **here** — not in weeks 1–8.

### Expect this, and say it out loud now

| Metric | Weeks 1–8 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement rate | **Falls** | Parents engage less than enthusiasts. This is the pivot working |
| Subscribers | Stalls, may go negative | Enthusiasts unsubscribe when the content stops being for them. Correct outcome |
| Views per video | Falls | Losing the audience that reliably watched |
| % US views | **Rises** | The metric that matters |
| % viewers 35–54 | **Rises** | The metric that matters |
| Conversions | Flat until ~week 9 | The pool has to exist before it can convert |

**If nobody is briefed on this table, the plan gets cancelled around week six** when the dashboard looks like a disaster. Circulate it before Phase 1, not after.

## 5. Measurement

### The channel state line

One line, reported weekly, this shape:

```
% US views · % viewers 35-54 · subs/1000 views · top-3 traffic sources · verdict
```

### Success metrics, ranked

1. **% views from US** — the pivot metric. Target: baseline → 60%+ by week 12
2. **% viewers 35–54** — the parent proxy. Target: 45%+ by week 12
3. **Channel-engager audience size (US, parent-age)** — the remarketing pool; the real commercial asset
4. **Click-through to LP** with UTM, per conversion-format video
5. **Trial signups attributed to `yt_organic`**
6. Subs per 1000 views — subscriber *quality*, not count

**Not success metrics:** engagement rate · total views · subscriber count · comment volume.

### The wrong-audience alarm

Checked at **72 hours** on every upload:

| Trip | Threshold | Means |
|---|---|---|
| Geo | India >35% of views | The packaging recruited the old audience |
| Age | 18–24 >30% of views | Enthusiast or student signal, not parent |
| Traffic | Suggested-from-own-old-videos is #1 source | The legacy library is still defining the channel |

A tripped alarm means **re-package** — new thumbnail, new title. It does not mean "make better content." Diagnosing a packaging failure as a content failure is how channels burn a year.

## 7. Open decisions — need Naina

**D1 · Channel consolidation.** `@CuemathStudio`, `@cuemath_academy`, and `Cuemath: Math Games & Classes` are all live. Which is the parent destination, and what happens to the others — redirect, fence, or leave running for a different job? Three audience graphs that don't compound is a worse problem than one mis-aimed graph. *Blocks: shelf rebuild.*

**D2 · Legacy library.** Recommendation: unlist all enthusiast content except the ~10 strongest, which go to one unfeatured playlist. Alternative: keep public but de-featured. Unlisting is the cleaner algorithmic signal; keeping public preserves whatever inbound links exist. *Blocks: Phase 1 item 1.*

**D3 · Paid seeding budget.** The plan does not work without it. Needs a number and an owner. Framed as audience acquisition — it will look terrible on a CPTD basis and should not be judged on one. *Blocks: Phase 2.*

**D4 · Handle.** "Studio" reads as a content brand, not a parent destination. Worth changing *if* D1 resolves toward this channel — but the rename buys naming clarity only, not an audience reset. *Does not block anything.*

**D5 · The new trust numbers.** `97.2%` · `2,500+ competition wins` · `<1% refund` · `4,000 tutors` · `80+ countries` are in the brand book but not in any verified-facts table. Cleared for organic channel copy on your confirmation; blocked in paid until then. `#1 on Trustpilot` stays out of Google Ads text regardless — platform superlative policy, not a brand call. See [[brand-book-v2]] §9.A. *Blocks: some playlist and About copy — drafted both ways.*

**D6 · Coach vs tutor on YouTube.** Proposed: `tutor` in titles and search-facing metadata (lexical match — YouTube search behaves like Google), `coach` on screen, in thumbnail copy, and in playlist titles (brand surface). Extends the existing surface rule rather than contradicting it. Needs sign-off before it is treated as locked. See [[brand-book-v2]] §9.J.
