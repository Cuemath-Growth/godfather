# Worked Example — How the Audit Runs

This is a complete example: a sample creative submitted in the input template format, followed by the full audit output. Use this to calibrate your expectations before your first real review.

## The Creative Submitted

```
COMMUNITY TARGET: Chinese-American (English-dominant)
FUNNEL STAGE: Awareness

CREATIVE CONCEPT:
Headline: "Math That Actually Challenges Your Child"
Body copy: "Most kids are bored in class — or struggling to keep up. Cuemath builds the kind of deep math foundation that turns great students into exceptional ones. Fun, engaging 1:1 sessions with our expert teachers. Book a free class today."
CTA: "Book a Free Class"

VISUAL DESCRIPTION:
Split image: left side shows a smiling Asian child at a desk with colorful worksheets. Right side shows a generic tutor icon with a star rating (4.9★).

DECISION MODEL THIS TARGETS (if you know):
DM1 — for parents who want their child to excel, not just keep up.

ANYTHING ELSE I SHOULD KNOW:
This is an awareness ad — first time these parents are seeing Cuemath.
```

Structural flag: CRITICAL. The CTA is "Book a Free Class" — a Step 5 commitment ask dropped into a Step 1 creative. A parent who has never heard of Cuemath and has been given zero specific proof about it will not book a class from this ad. The CTA mismatch alone will suppress click-through regardless of copy quality. This is the primary structural failure.

━━ DECISION MODEL ━━
Primary DM: DM1 — Acceleration/Competitive Track ("turns great students into exceptional ones")
Modifier: None identified

## What a Passing Creative Looks Like

For contrast, here is what a SHIP WITH MODIFICATIONS result looks like for a rebuilt concept targeting the same audience:

**Rebuilt creative (Chinese-American, Awareness, Step 1 — Recognition):**
- Headline: "Your Grade 4 child should be solving problems school won't cover until Grade 6."
- Body: "Cuemath's curriculum runs 2 years ahead of Common Core — because grade-level math isn't enough for the parents in our program. A structured track, a named tutor who stays with your child, and a diagnostic that shows exactly where they are on Day 1. See what Grade 4 covers."
- Visual: Curriculum comparison — three columns: "US Common Core Grade 4," "Grade 4 school math," "Cuemath Grade 4" — with specific topic names visible (e.g., "Distributive property," "Algebraic notation," "Variables").
- CTA: "See the Grade 4 curriculum →"

**Why this passes the audit:**
- Staircase: Step 1 (Recognition) with a Step 1 CTA — "See the curriculum" asks for nothing more than interest. Correct match.
- DM1 positioned with proof: "2 years ahead of Common Core" is a specific, verifiable ceiling claim. "Grade-level math isn't enough" signals this is for parents whose child is already doing fine but needs more.
- Seriousness Demand 1 (structured path): comparison chart shows a defined curriculum track.
- Seriousness Demand 2 (real rigor): "2 years ahead" is specific enough to be credible, not a generic claim.
- Seriousness Demand 4 (trustworthy tutor): "named tutor who stays with your child" is referenced — not generic.
- Scroll-stop: "Your Grade 4 child should be solving problems school won't cover until Grade 6" is a claim that stops a DM1 parent because it's specific, surprising, and verifiable.
- Click reason: The parent reads "2 years ahead" and wants to know what specifically. The curriculum chart is behind the click. Specific question, specific answer.
- No dismissal triggers: No "fun," no "engaging," no generic tutor icon, no vague progress claim.

**Meta Effectiveness Test:**
- 2-second test: Headline is specific and stops DM1 scroll. Comparison chart visual shows substance immediately. Yes, this stops scroll.
- Click reason: "See what Grade 4 covers" — parent has a specific question (what IS 2 years ahead?) that the click answers. Yes, click reason exists.
- Dismissal triggers: None identified.
- Trial probability: High relative to the original — earns a click from the correct parent, moves them to Step 2 (Trust) content.

**Remaining modifications before ship (SHIP WITH MODIFICATIONS):**
1. "Grade-level math isn't enough for the parents in our program" — slightly othering to DM2 parents who might be in the targeting. Reframe as: "Grade-level math isn't enough for families who want to stay ahead." Keeps DM1 focus without excluding.
2. Comparison chart must show real Cuemath Grade 4 content — verify specific topic names with Joyita/Acads before briefing design. Do not approximate.
