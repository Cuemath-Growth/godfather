# Reading Library — Pro Marketer / Team Leadership / AI as Multiplier

Curated entry points for a senior performance + brand marketer at Cuemath. One best link per source, what the source argues, and the operationally useful nugget for the specific job — running paid acquisition + brand across US/NRI/India/AUS/UK/MEA on Meta + Google.

---

## Part I — Pro Marketer Fundamentals

### 1. Byron Sharp — *How Brands Grow* (Ehrenberg-Bass)
- **Entry point:** [Brand Genetics — *How Brands Grow* speed summary](https://brandgenetics.com/human-thinking/how-brands-grow-speed-summary/) (free; the book itself is the canonical source but the summary is the highest-signal 20-min way in)
- **Core argument:** Brand growth is driven by **penetration** (more buyers), not loyalty. Two levers do most of the work: **mental availability** (does the brand come to mind in the buying moment?) and **physical availability** (can they actually find/buy it?). Distinctive brand assets — logos, colours, characters, jingles — are the carriers of mental availability; consistency over years compounds.
- **For Cuemath:** Stop optimising RSAs for "loyalty-style" intent variants. The leverage is widening the funnel — broad-reach creative that makes a parent think "Cuemath" when their kid struggles with maths in 6 months. That's why a consistent MathFit visual + coach-on-camera asset across markets matters more than any one ad's CTR.

### 2. Les Binet & Peter Field — *The Long and the Short of It* (60/40 rule)
- **Entry point:** [IPA — The Key Works of Les Binet & Peter Field](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/effectiveness-research-analysis/les-binet-peter-field) (free overview + report downloads); Alex Murrell's [chapter-by-chapter summary](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/les-binet-and-peter-field-the-long-and-the-short-of-it) is the fastest read.
- **Core argument:** Across 996 IPA case studies, the campaigns that grew long-term share split spend ~60% brand-building / 40% short-term activation. Brand drives base sales and pricing power; activation harvests existing intent. Get the ratio wrong and you optimise yourself into stagnation — high short-term ROAS, falling CAC efficiency over 2-3 years.
- **For Cuemath:** Cuemath's spend is almost certainly >80% activation (Meta + Google direct-response). The 60/40 ratio is a North Star, not a rule to apply literally — but the diagnostic question is real: what fraction of next quarter's spend is building mental availability for *next year's* parent vs. harvesting this week's high-intent searcher?

### 3. Mark Ritson — Mini MBA / Diagnosis → Strategy → Tactics
- **Entry point:** [Mark Ritson — "The missing step in marketing plans: brand diagnosis"](https://minimba.com/the-missing-step-in-marketing-plans-brand-diagnosis/) (free; the most-cited single Ritson piece)
- **Core argument:** Most marketers skip diagnosis and go straight to tactics. The discipline is three sequential stages — **Diagnosis** (research the market, the consumer, the brand) → **Strategy** (who/where/why: targeting, positioning, objectives) → **Tactics** (the 4Ps). Strategy is *choosing what not to do*. If you can't write your diagnosis on a page, your strategy is fiction.
- **For Cuemath:** Before the next RSA refresh / LP wave, run the Ritson order on each market segment (US-NRI, US-Domestic, India, ANZ, MEA, UK) — five-line diagnosis → one-line strategy → tactics. Stops the trap of testing copy variants when the actual problem is positioning. (Note: the user described this as "D-O-S"; Ritson's published framework is D-S-T with Objectives nested inside Strategy — same idea, different acronym.)

### 4. Rory Sutherland — *Alchemy*
- **Entry point:** [TED — *Perspective is Everything* (16 min)](https://www.ted.com/talks/rory_sutherland_perspective_is_everything) (free; tightest possible distillation of the Alchemy worldview)
- **Core argument:** Humans don't behave according to economic logic — they behave according to **psycho-logic**. The biggest gains in business come from changing perception, framing, and context, not from improving the product itself. Small, "irrational" details (the name of the seat, the wait-time display, the colour of the box) move behaviour more than rational optimisation.
- **For Cuemath:** The "$20/class" price isn't a number — it's a frame. Same with "Cuemath Coach," "MathFit," "1-on-1." Sutherland would push you to test perception levers (naming the trial "Diagnostic," reframing 55 minutes as "a full school period," recasting price-per-class as price-per-hour-of-IIT-tutor-time) before another headline A/B test.

### 5. Eric Seufert — Mobile Dev Memo (paid acquisition strategy)
- **Entry point:** [Mobile Dev Memo — "Mobile User Acquisition 101"](https://mobiledevmemo.com/mobile-user-acquisition-101/) (free) for fundamentals; then [*The profound, unintended consequence of ATT: content fortresses*](https://mobiledevmemo.com/the-profound-unintended-consequence-of-att-content-fortresses/) (free) for the strategic worldview shift.
- **Core argument:** Post-ATT, attribution is structurally broken — last-click is an illusion, and the platforms that own the user (Meta, Google, TikTok) have become "content fortresses" that you optimise *into*, not through. Modern UA is portfolio optimisation against opaque algorithms: incrementality testing, MMM, and creative volume become the real levers — not micro-targeting.
- **For Cuemath:** Two structural takeaways. (1) Treat per-ad CPTQL with suspicion; the algorithm is reallocating in ways your dashboard can't see — Godfather's TD-threshold gates already lean this way, keep leaning. (2) Creative volume + diversification of distinctive assets beats narrower targeting; lean into the creative-tagging + match-engine loop you're building.

### 6. Bob Hoffman — *Marketing Interrupted* / The Ad Contrarian
- **Entry point:** [More About Advertising — *Bob Hoffman: why I'm no longer very interested in today's advertising*](https://www.moreaboutadvertising.com/2023/01/ad-contrarian-bob-hoffman-why-im-no-longer-very-interested-in-todays-advertising/) (free, dense interview that hits every Hoffman position in one read)
- **Core argument:** The ad industry has confused activity with effectiveness. Programmatic / behavioural targeting is largely a fraud-and-waste machine; consumers don't want "relationships" with brands; creativity has been gutted in favour of data theatre. The historically reliable levers — distinctive creative, broad reach, memorable brands — still work; everything else is marketing-industrial-complex noise.
- **For Cuemath:** Healthy counterweight to Meta/Google rep narratives. When a platform pitches a new AI bidding product or audience signal, Hoffman's stance is: *prove incrementality or assume waste*. Pair with Seufert for the rigorous version of the same skepticism.

## Part II — Team Leadership / Institutional Knowledge Transfer

### 7. Andy Grove — *High Output Management*
- **Entry point:** [Ian Tien — "Top Takeaways from Andy Grove's *High Output Management*" (Medium)](https://medium.com/@iantien/top-takeaways-from-andy-grove-s-high-output-management-2e0ecfb1ea63) (free; the canonical short summary, frequently cited by Andreessen, Horowitz, etc.)
- **Core argument:** A manager's output = the output of their team + the output of teams they influence. The job is therefore high-leverage activities: information gathering, decision making, nudging — not doing the work yourself. OKRs cascade ("Where am I going? How will I know?"). Training and 1:1s are the highest-leverage things on your calendar.
- **For Cuemath:** When you scale from solo content designer to a team of 3-5, the trap is doing the writing yourself because it's faster *this week*. Grove's frame: the hour spent writing a creative brief template that 4 people use for a year is 200x leverage vs. writing one ad yourself.

### 8. Will Larson — *An Elegant Puzzle* + "Good engineering strategy is boring"
- **Entry point:** [Will Larson — *Write five, then synthesize: good engineering strategy is boring*](https://lethain.com/good-engineering-strategy-is-boring/) (free; this is the specific essay)
- **Core argument:** Good strategy is **honest diagnosis + practical approach** — boring, specific, and grounded in real constraints. The method: write five concrete design docs solving real problems, then pull the recurring tradeoffs out into a strategy doc, then extrapolate to a 2-3 year vision. Bottom-up, not top-down. Abstract strategies fail because two well-meaning people interpret them differently.
- **For Cuemath:** Directly portable to marketing strategy. Instead of writing a "Q3 NRI Strategy" deck, write five real campaign briefs (Mother's Day NRI, US-HS test-prep, AUS Selective, India LinkedIn LP, MEA Cambridge) — then synthesise what's recurring. That's your actual strategy. This is the antidote to slide-deck strategy that nobody operationalises.

### 9. Camille Fournier — *The Manager's Path*
- **Entry point:** [Shortform — *The Manager's Path* book overview](https://www.shortform.com/blog/the-managers-path-camille-fournier/) (free summary; full book is the canonical read but the summary captures 80%)
- **Core argument:** The path from IC → tech lead → manager → director → executive is a sequence of role *changes*, not promotions. Three durable skills at every level: clear communication (especially 1:1s and written async), strategic planning (10 productive weeks per quarter, 20% sustaining work), adaptability. Each stage requires letting go of the previous stage's source of competence.
- **For Cuemath:** When you hire your first direct report, the temptation is to keep doing the IC work + manage on the side. Fournier's "10 weeks not 13" budgeting rule applies cleanly to a marketing team — quarterly planning that assumes 100% capacity is fiction.

### 10. Julie Zhuo — *The Making of a Manager*
- **Entry point:** [Fellow.ai — *Top 10 Lessons from Julie Zhuo's The Making of a Manager*](https://fellow.ai/blog/julie-zhuo-the-making-of-a-manager/) (free; tightest summary online)
- **Core argument:** Management is three things: **Purpose, People, Process**. A great manager is judged by team output, full stop. First 90 days = relationships first, then a small starter project, then a first design iteration. Feedback should be task-specific, same-day, and frequent — not saved for reviews.
- **For Cuemath:** Pairs with Fournier — Zhuo is warmer and more design-team-flavoured (she ran Facebook product design), Fournier is more rigorous and tech-flavoured. Read Zhuo for the people-side scripts (how to give feedback to someone whose draft is bad), Fournier for the structure.

### 11. Lenny Rachitsky — newsletter (3-5 highest-signal pieces for an AI-augmented team)
- **Entry points (all free):**
  - [*How AI will impact product management*](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-ai-will-impact-product-management) — the strategic frame
  - [*How to become a supermanager with AI*](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-become-a-supermanager-with) — concrete manager-level workflows
  - [*Five proven prompt engineering techniques (and a few more-advanced tactics)*](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/five-proven-prompt-engineering-techniques) — the craft layer
  - [*You should be playing with GPTs at work*](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/you-should-be-playing-with-gpts-at) — case for skill-style modular AI
  - [*Why PMs are best positioned to thrive in an AI world*](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/why-pms-are-best-positioned-to-thrive) — applies 1:1 to marketing PMs
- **Core argument across the set:** The PM/operator skills that compound in an AI world are taste, prioritisation, prompt quality, and judgment on when to verify. AI shrinks the cost of generation; the bottleneck shifts to selection. Modular reusable AI assets (GPTs / skills / prompts) beat one-off prompts.
- **For Cuemath:** Direct read-across — your `/write`, `/brief`, `/radar`, `/godfather` slash commands are exactly the "GPTs at work" pattern Lenny describes. The supermanager piece is the highest-signal one when you're sizing your first hire.

## Part III — AI as Team Multiplier

### 12. Anthropic Skills repo (`anthropics/skills`) — skill-creator + eval loop
- **Entry point:** [`skills/skill-creator/SKILL.md`](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/skill-creator/SKILL.md) on GitHub (free)
- **Core argument:** Skills are folders with a SKILL.md and YAML frontmatter — packaged instructions Claude loads on demand. The skill-creator's contribution is the **eval loop**: write 2-3 realistic test prompts, save to `evals/evals.json`, run with-skill vs. baseline in parallel, generate the eval-viewer HTML *before* you grade outputs yourself, let a human review, iterate up to 5 times, pick the version with the best held-out test score (not train score, to avoid overfitting).
- **For Cuemath:** This is the playbook for hardening every Brain folder skill (Forge, Scout, Godfather, Radar, Tolstoy, Curator). Right now they're written, not evaluated. The eval loop pattern would turn "did this `/write meta` give me a good reel?" from gut-feel into a 5-run benchmark you can iterate against — exactly the rigour you already apply to creative tagging.

### 13. Addy Osmani — `addyosmani/agent-skills` (context-engineering + doubt-driven-development)
- **Entry point:** [`skills/using-agent-skills/SKILL.md`](https://github.com/addyosmani/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/using-agent-skills/SKILL.md) (the meta-skill that orients the rest; free); then the two specific skills — [`context-engineering/SKILL.md`](https://github.com/addyosmani/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/context-engineering/SKILL.md) and [`doubt-driven-development/SKILL.md`](https://github.com/addyosmani/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/doubt-driven-development/SKILL.md)
- **Core argument:** A library of 22 lifecycle skills (Define → Plan → Build → Verify → Review → Ship) + 1 meta-skill, designed for senior-engineer rigour. **Context-engineering** = load the right context at the right time (rules files, packed context, MCP integrations) instead of flooding the agent with everything. **Doubt-driven-development** = an adversarial fresh-perspective review of every non-trivial in-flight decision via the loop CLAIM > EXTRACT > DOUBT > RECONCILE > STOP, with optional cross-model escalation.
- **For Cuemath:** Context-engineering directly explains the Brain folder's MEMORY.md + skill loading pattern — and why the May 13 "skills are thinking tools, not templates" learning matters. Doubt-driven-development is the missing piece on creative ship-readiness: Radar already gates ship-readiness, but a doubt-driven step ("what's the strongest counter-argument to this hook?") before high-stakes creative would prevent the May 12 NRI / MathFit / parent-narrating misses.

### 14. Ethan Mollick — *Co-Intelligence* + *One Useful Thing* (2-3 most useful for a one-person AI-augmented marketing function)
- **Book entry point:** [*Co-Intelligence* (Penguin)](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/741805/co-intelligence-by-ethan-mollick/) — the four rules: Always invite AI to the table; Be the human in the loop; Treat AI like a person but tell it what kind of person; Assume this is the worst AI you will ever use.
- **Best free posts for a solo operator:**
  - [*Using AI Right Now: A Quick Guide*](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/using-ai-right-now-a-quick-guide) — the practical "what tools, in what order" — Mollick updates this every few months, treat it as your tool-selection North Star.
  - [*On Working with Wizards*](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/on-working-with-wizards) — the most important recent piece: AI is shifting from collaborator (co-intelligence) to "wizard" (you prompt, you wait, you verify but can't really evaluate). The risk: every task you outsource is a task you no longer build judgment in.
  - [*Why PMs are best positioned to thrive in an AI world*](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/why-pms-are-best-positioned-to-thrive) — guest essay; pairs cleanly with Mollick's worldview (cross-referenced from Lenny above).
- **For Cuemath:** The *Wizards* essay is the single most operationally important read on this list for you specifically. You are a data-analyst-first marketer running an AI-augmented stack — the danger isn't that AI is bad at the job, it's that as it gets *better*, you lose the ability to spot when it's wrong. The countermeasure is the discipline you already practise (verified facts, banned claims, pre-write grounding) — Mollick makes the case for why that discipline *increases* in value, not decreases, as the models improve.

## How to read this list

If you only read four things this quarter:
1. **Larson — "good engineering strategy is boring"** (free essay, 15 min) — fixes how you write any strategy doc.
2. **Ritson — "the missing step: brand diagnosis"** (free essay, 10 min) — fixes what goes in the strategy doc.
3. **Mollick — "On Working with Wizards"** (free essay, 12 min) — fixes how you stay sharp around AI.
4. **Anthropic skill-creator SKILL.md** (free, 20 min) — fixes how the Brain folder evolves from "written" to "evaluated."

Everything else compounds on those four.
