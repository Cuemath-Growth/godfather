# Voice canon — MEA international expat parent

The register for MEA creative. Single canon — international expat register. Indian / South Asian diaspora + Western expat (UK / US / European) as primary targets. Arab families in international schools as nominal target (low conversion historically).

Per the May 12 strategic call: NO "Indian" framing in copy. Global brand voice. NO vernacular phrases anywhere — even at parent-emotional-anchor moments. The MEA expat audience is multi-cultural and multi-nationality; any vernacular markers (Hindi, Arabic, Tagalog) push out at least two-thirds of the audience.

---

## Who this parent actually is

She is 30-45. She is on an expat work-permit or family-visa to the UAE (Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah), Saudi Arabia (Riyadh, Jeddah), Qatar (Doha), Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman. Her family has been in the region anywhere from 6 months to 15 years.

She is one of:
- **Indian / South Asian diaspora** (Indian, Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Sri Lankan) — the largest cohort. Often working in IT, banking, retail, healthcare, education. Lives in Bur Dubai, Karama, Al Barsha, International City (Dubai); Sharjah Al Nahda; Abu Dhabi Al Reef.
- **Western expat** (UK, US, European) — works in international companies, often executive-level. Lives in The Greens, JLT, Dubai Marina, Arabian Ranches, Springs, Meadows.
- **Arab family in international schools** — local Emirati, Saudi, Qatari, Lebanese, Jordanian families who choose IB / Cambridge / American schools over Arabic-medium government schools. Smaller cohort, historically low Cuemath conversion. Nominal target only.

Her kids are at international schools — IB World Schools, Cambridge-curriculum schools (IGCSE pathway), American schools (high school), British schools (GCSE / A-Level pathway, but K-8 means we only target the lower half). KHDA (Knowledge and Human Development Authority — Dubai's school regulator) is part of her vocabulary.

She speaks English at work, English in her social life. Her home language exists at her parents' house (if visiting), at religious gatherings, in WhatsApp with her own mother. Her kids are bicultural — international school exposure means they often speak English better than any other language.

Her primary anxiety: navigating curriculum transitions (KS3 → IGCSE in British schools, MYP → IBDP in IB schools, US Middle School → US High School in American schools). She's also navigating different country-of-origin university aspirations — Russell Group UK, Ivy League US, IIT/NIT India, Australian Group of Eight, NUS / NTU Singapore. This audience often plans across multiple education systems simultaneously.

She trusts evidence + curriculum-fit + operational reliability. Less community-network-trust (her social density of co-nationals is lower than Indian-Australian or UK British-Indian audiences). More platform-and-product evaluation.

She compares Cuemath against international online tutoring (MyTutor UK, Cluey-style platforms, Brightspark, Tutor House) and against edtech apps (Bhanzu UAE presence, BYJU'S, Vedantu). Local KHDA-approved tuition centres exist but are usually subject-broad not maths-deep. Local 1:1 home tutors are common in dense diaspora communities — same wedge as India + UK + AU (method-visibility differentiator).

## Register markers — how the sentences are shaped

**Neutral international English.** Not American-fluent, not British-fluent specifically. Educated, multinational, register-agnostic. The kind of English used in international business + international school parent communication.

**BrE spelling default.** Personalised · centre · colour · recognise · programme · maths · Mum · favourite · honour. International schools mostly use BrE spelling conventions. Mixed-spelling environments (some American international schools) — default to BrE unless the specific LP audience cell is American-school targeted.

**Curriculum specificity.** Use the exact framework names — IB Primary Years Programme (PYP), Middle Years Programme (MYP), Diploma Programme (DP). Cambridge International curriculum, IGCSE. American Common Core, AP. British National Curriculum, KS1-KS3 (KS4-5 out of K-8 cap for MEA).

**No cultural-language markers.** No Hindi, no Arabic, no Tagalog, no specific Asian-language phrases — even at influencer-parent-quote moments. The multi-cultural audience means any specific vernacular pushes out two-thirds of viewers.

**Service-standards register.** International school parents are operationally fluent. They expect responsive counsellor calls, accurate tutor reports, working platform, predictable scheduling. Operational friction is grounds for churn.

**Cross-country aspiration framing.** Many MEA expat parents are planning across multiple education systems simultaneously (return to India, move to UK, kid going to US college, etc.). Brand voice should accommodate this — "maths that builds across curricula and across years" lands.

**Long summer / break-driven cadence.** UAE summer break is 3 months (Jun-Sep). UK / US school year still operates. International schools start in late August / early September. The cadence is unusual — copy and creative should reflect this rhythm.

## Sample lines that land

✅ *"Year 5 to Year 8. One Cuemath teacher. Across IB, Cambridge, American, British — the maths foundation is the same."*
— Curriculum-agnostic framing. Longitudinal proof. International-school-fluent vocabulary.

✅ *"Same teacher across two schools. We moved from Sharjah American to GEMS Wellington last year — the teacher stayed. The continuity is what made the move work."*
— Cross-school continuity. International school name. Honest moving-family narrative.

✅ *"The MYP-to-IBDP gap is real. We started Cuemath at the end of Year 8 specifically to close it before she starts DP next year."*
— Curriculum transition fluency. Specific framework names. Aspirational planning.

✅ *"Same teacher every session. Reports detailed enough that I know what they did without asking. Counsellor calls when she says she will. That's what 'reliable' actually means in a tutoring service."*
— Operational reliability as proof. Multiple service-standards markers. Honest qualification of "reliable."

✅ *"My son was ahead in his school in Mumbai. Slightly behind at his school in Dubai. Cuemath knows both curricula — bridged the gap in six months."*
— Cross-country narrative. Specific home country + UAE city. Curriculum-fluency proof.

## Sample lines that BREAK the voice

❌ *"नमस्ते! Help your child master maths with our personalised tutors!"*
— Vernacular markers in MEA copy (Hindi greeting). Pushes out two-thirds of audience. Plus exclamation point.

❌ *"Crush the IGCSE with our hand-picked specialists!"*
— Crush (American slang), hand-picked (bleached), specialists (banned), exclamation point off-register.

❌ *"Our Indian-trained tutors bring the rigour of Indian education to Dubai families."*
— Indian-trained (banned globally), rigour-of-Indian-education (cultural framing), narrow audience targeting in a multi-cultural creative.

❌ *"Watch your child fall in love with maths!"*
— Banned phrase pattern. Exclamation point.

❌ *"Trusted by hundreds of expat families across the UAE."*
— Generic "hundreds" (no specificity), trusted (bleached). Replace with school-name specificity: "Families with kids at GEMS Wellington, Dubai American Academy, JESS Jumeirah are switching to Cuemath."

❌ *"Our specialists deliver guaranteed grade improvement in IGCSE!"*
— Specialists (banned), guaranteed (forbidden — we never promise outcomes), exclamation point.

## Anti-patterns specific to MEA international expat

1. **Vernacular markers anywhere in copy.** No Hindi, Arabic, Tagalog. Single-cultural-marker creative pushes out the multi-cultural audience.
2. **Indian-credentialing leads.** Banned globally + specifically resistant from multi-cultural MEA audience.
3. **American register imported.** "Crush," "killed it," exclamation points — wrong register for the international-school parent.
4. **Single-curriculum framing.** "We help with IGCSE" — only addresses Cambridge families. Better: "We help across IB / Cambridge / American / British."
5. **AmE spelling on MEA creative.** Default to BrE. Major audience overlap with British-International-School families who notice the spelling difference.
6. **Outcome-led visuals.** Per MEA visual canon — verified MEA outcome bank is thin. Don't lead with certificate / score / placement visuals (you don't have enough to substantiate). Use Trustpilot quotes + curriculum-fluency proof instead.
7. **KHDA / Arabic-curriculum framing.** Cuemath doesn't currently serve Arabic-medium schools. Don't claim KHDA-mainstream presence; focus on KHDA-international-schools subset.
8. **Performative cultural celebration.** Ramadan + Eid ads with festive imagery + Arabic greetings — patronising and miscalibrated for the international-expat target. Cultural moments work quietly (e.g. accommodate Ramadan iftar timing in trial scheduling), not as headline.
9. **Geographic over-specificity.** "Dubai parents" works. "Jumeirah Beach parents" too narrow. "Sharjah Indian-school parents" — works only when targeting that specific cell.
10. **GCSE / A-Level / IBDP references.** Out of K-8 cap. Cuemath MEA serves PYP / KS1-KS3 / MYP Years 1-3 / American K-8. The transitions INTO GCSE / IBDP are scope; the exams themselves are not.
