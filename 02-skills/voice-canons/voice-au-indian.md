# Voice canon — Indian-Australian parent

The register for Indian-Australian creative. ~900,000 India-born Aussies + 800,000+ with Indian ancestry — largest non-Anglo cohort in Australia, youngest immigrant group, highest education levels. NSW + VIC concentration.

Per the May 12 strategic call: NO "Indian" framing in copy. Global brand voice. Vernacular phrases at parent-emotional-anchor moments allowed (similar to US NRI + UK British-Indian canons). Indian-Australian audience is the "NRI Returner" archetype from Trial Mastery AU §U1.

---

## Who this parent actually is

She is 32-46. Either she moved to Australia within the last 10-15 years (recent), or she came as a young child / was born here (settled). Sydney (largest cohort — Parramatta, Blacktown, Strathfield, Wentworthville, Castle Hill, Hornsby), Melbourne (Truganina, Tarneit, Point Cook, Caroline Springs, Glen Waverley), Brisbane (Sunnybank, Calamvale), Perth (Canning Vale, Thornlie), or Adelaide (Mawson Lakes, Salisbury).

Her kids are in NSW / VIC / QLD / WA / SA government schools, sometimes private/independent. NSW has the OC / Selective testing pipeline that defines her academic anxiety — Y4 sits OC, Y6 sits Selective. VIC has scholarship exams (ACER, Edutest). Other states have GATE testing (WA) or state-specific scholarship pathways.

She speaks English at work, English with her kids. Hindi / Tamil / Telugu / Malayalam / Punjabi / Bengali / Gujarati / Marathi at home with her parents and at emotional moments. The Indian-Australian cohort is linguistically diverse — Punjabi is the 6th most-spoken language in Australia.

Her primary anxiety is the OC Test (Y4, NSW), the Selective Schools Test (Y6, NSW), Scholarship exams (Y5-6, multi-state). NAPLAN is also in scope (Y3/5/7/9 March, nationwide) but a lower-stakes baseline than the selective tests.

She trusts community referral but in a more dispersed network than UK/US — diaspora community in NSW Indian Senior Citizens, Sydney Tamil Sangam, Sankat Mochan temples, the WhatsApp parent group for her kid's grade. *Word travels but slower than in US Texas temples.*

She compares Cuemath against Kumon (heavy AU presence, centre-based group worksheets) and Cluey Learning (AU-native online 1:1 — closest direct competitor — but rotating tutors). Bhanzu has YouTube presence among Indian-Australians. Local Indian-origin home tutors exist in dense diaspora pockets but tend toward homework-help, not Selective/OC prep.

## Register markers — how the sentences are shaped

**Australian English everywhere.** "maths" (NOT math). "Mum" (NOT mom). "Year 5/6" (NOT Grade 5/6). "Tutor" or "tutoring" (UK "tuition" rarer in AU but understood).

**BrE spelling.** Personalised · centre · colour · recognise · programme · favourite. Same as UK.

**OC / Selective / Scholarship system fluency.** Use the exact test names: OC Test (capital T), Selective Schools Test, Scholarship exams. Year 4 for OC, Year 6 for Selective. NSW specificity (most OC/Selective is NSW) unless explicitly multi-state context.

**State-specific where relevant.** NSW (Sydney) audience is the largest — OC/Selective lead. VIC (Melbourne) audience is the second-largest — Scholarship lead (ACER, Edutest). WA = GATE testing. QLD = Academic Excellence Programs. SA = smaller, more general.

**Diaspora-community-trust framing.** "A family at our community group" / "Two kids from her Bharatanatyam class" / "We heard about it from a mum at the temple." The Indian-Australian diaspora references run through specific community institutions — Tamil Sangam, Sankat Mochan, Khalsa schools, gurdwaras, BAPS temples.

**Vernacular at felt moments only.** Same rule as UK + US NRI canons — emotional-anchor lines, single-instance per script. NOT in brand body.

**Long-form patience.** Indian-Australian mum reads. Will engage with a 60-second video, a 4-fold LP, a long FAQ. Speed bias is for second-gen US (and AU East Asian — separate canon).

## Sample lines that land

✅ *"Year 4 to Year 7. One Cuemath teacher. The OC and Selective are folded into the school maths year-round."*
— Year-label fluency. Longitudinal frame. Anti-specialist positioning. Specific AU test names.

✅ *"My daughter's been with her Cuemath teacher for two years. She did the OC last year — top half. Now she's prepping the Selective with the same teacher."*
— Tenure proof. Specific AU exam named. Honest outcome (top half, not "topped the test"). Continuity narrative.

✅ *"She's strong on the arithmetic but panics on time. Cuemath teacher's been working on her timing for the OC — without making her hate the practice."*
— Specific cognitive shift (timing). AU test referenced. Behavioural improvement (without hating it).

✅ *"Two families from our community group are on Cuemath now. One had her son at James Ruse after the Selective, the other's daughter is at North Sydney Girls."*
— Community-trust origin story. Named selective high schools (proof of placement). AU-specific schools.

✅ *"The teacher is patient. Lets him think. Doesn't jump in with the answer."*
— Trustpilot-mined word ("patient"). Specific method observation (lets him think). Short sentence rhythm.

## Sample lines that BREAK the voice

❌ *"Help your child crush the OC Test with our 11+ specialists!"*
— Multiple failures: "11+ specialists" (wrong country — that's UK), "crush" (American slang), exclamation point.

❌ *"Watch your child fall in love with math!"*
— Wrong spelling (math not maths), exclamation point off-register, "fall in love" framing American.

❌ *"Our Indian-trained tutors bring the rigour of Indian education to Australian classrooms."*
— "Indian-trained" banned globally + specifically retired. AU audience increasingly skeptical of Indian-origin branding.

❌ *"Our hand-picked specialists deliver guaranteed Selective placement!"*
— Hand-picked (bleached), specialists (banned), guaranteed (forbidden — we don't promise outcomes), exclamation point.

❌ *"Trusted by hundreds of mums across Sydney."*
— Generic "hundreds" (no specificity), trusted (bleached). Replace with named cohort: "Families from the Sydney Tamil Sangam are on Cuemath."

❌ *"Get an edge on NAPLAN this March!"*
— "Get an edge" American, NAPLAN-anchored creative in March = at-the-exam (should peak Nov-Feb pre-window per [[feedback_session_may12_meta_failures]]), exclamation point.

- *"Maths for Year 5 and Year 6. Properly taught. NSW Selective is one chapter."*
- *"The maths programme for OC, Selective and Scholarship — built around your child's actual school year."*

## Anti-patterns specific to Indian-Australian

1. **Indian-credentialing leads.** "Indian-trained tutors," "rigour of Indian education." Banned globally + specifically resistant from this audience.
2. **American register imported.** "Crush," "killed it," "math whiz," "all-star." All wrong. AU register is understated like UK.
3. **UK 11+ vocabulary on AU creative.** "11+" is a UK term — Australia uses OC + Selective. Critical fluency signal.
4. **Wrong test attribution to wrong state.** OC is NSW-only (and a few other states). Selective is NSW + some others. Scholarship is multi-state but specific exam bodies (ACER, Edutest). Don't say "OC Test in Victoria" — VIC has its own Scholarship pathway.
5. **NAPLAN out of season.** NAPLAN-anchored copy only Nov-Feb (prep window). Pause Mar-Oct. NAPLAN in Apr-Oct = wasted CPM.
6. **AmE spelling.** "Personalized" wrong; should be "Personalised." "Color" wrong; "Colour." 
7. **"Grade 5" / "5th grade".** Wrong system. AU uses "Year 5."
8. **"Mom" instead of "Mum".** Immediate dialect mismatch.
9. **Exclamation points + hype.** Off-register entirely. AU Indian register is calm-considered.
10. **Performative cultural celebration.** Diwali ads with bhangra dancers — patronising. Cultural moments land quietly (e.g. tenured-tutor-relationship over the Diwali break) not as headline.
11. **Vernacular in brand body.** Hindi / Tamil / Punjabi / Gujarati phrases at influencer parent-quote moments only. NOT in body of Meta static or LP hero.
12. **Generic "hundreds of mums" claims.** AU audience wants school-name specificity (James Ruse, North Sydney Girls, Sydney Girls, Sydney Boys, Baulkham Hills Selective, North Sydney Boys). Generic = no trust.
