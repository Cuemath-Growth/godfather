# Voice canon — Asian-American Mom Creator (non-NRI East Asian)

The register for non-NRI East Asian-American Mom Creator scripts — Chinese, Korean, Filipino, Vietnamese, Taiwanese mothers in the US. School-pickup conversational register. Codified from Naina's direct Erden rewrite (May 10-11) + 14-draft iteration session.

This is the most-codified canon Cuemath has. Use it as the model for how voice canons get developed.

---

## Who this creator actually is

She is 32-45. Grew up in the US or came as a young child. Lives in California (Bay Area, OC, LA), Pacific NW, NJ, NY suburbs, Atlanta — wherever East Asian-American communities have density. She is a working mum or stay-at-home mum with 1-3 kids in elementary or middle school.

She runs a content account on Instagram or TikTok. 50K-500K followers. Her audience is other East Asian-American moms. She posts about parenting, school-related logistics, what's working for her family, products she actually uses. She does NOT post professional-style brand-deck content. Her followers are her *peers*, not her *fans*.

When she sponsors a brand, she has to keep her audience's trust. The moment her sponsored content sounds scripted, her engagement drops, her audience tunes out, and she loses leverage. Her brand-deal money depends on her continuing to sound like *herself*.

She is NOT NRI. The South-Asian-rigor register reads constructed to her. She doesn't have a temple network or a WhatsApp community. She has school-pickup conversations, mum group texts, and her own Instagram comments.

## Register markers — 12 tonal observations

From the canonical Erden rewrite. NOT a checklist. Read to develop ear, write from inside the register, then check.

1. **Conversational connectors start sentences.** "So," "But," "Though," "And" — for spoken flow, not written precision.
2. **Repetition for emphasis on load-bearing claims.** Restate the most important claim twice with slight rephrasing. *"The same tutor continues throughout. So it's the same person teaching every single class."*
3. **Parent vocabulary, not education-system vocabulary.** "School curriculum" not "what his grade covers this year." "His math skills" not "where he is academically." If a phrase sounds like a school district's letter, cut it.
4. **Conversational fillers earn their keep.** "Actually," "directly," "anymore" — spoken-English rhythm markers.
5. **Positive framing throughout.** Lead with what IS. Negation ("not just," "didn't just") only when the positive needs a contrast.
6. **Specificity to the creator's real life.** "Both my kids at their level" because Erden has two. "As a mom of six" because Solana has six. Generic substitutes ("them," "the kids") give up authority.
7. **CTA loops back to the hook.** Hook → "kids finish math homework in fifteen minutes" → CTA → "If your kid is fast at math, know for real where their understanding is at."
8. **MathFit lives on closing card alone.** Body shows what MathFit looks like; closing card carries the term.
9. **Em-dashes for cadence breaks.** Not commas, not periods. The em-dash carries a parent's spoken pause.
10. **Sentence rhythm varied.** Short + long + short. Never uniform stamps in a row.
11. **No performed emotion.** Matter-of-fact reporting. Cut "blown away," "game-changer," "honestly that confidence? That's everything."
12. **No standalone explainer scene.** No "Whether your child is in K-12, Cuemath helps them..." Drop broadcast intros.

## Sample lines that land

✅ *"There was one name though that kept coming up as different — Cuemath, and it's completely personalized."*
— Conversational connector ("though"). Em-dash. "Different" as anchor. "Completely personalized" — note: per May 13 audit, "personalized" is now allowed.

✅ *"He's quick. He gets the answer fast. But ask him why."*
— Short + short + short with a tonal pivot at "but." Specific cognitive shift named.

✅ *"As a mom of six, I needed something that didn't require my babysitting."*
— Creator-life specificity (six kids). Practical-problem language. No marketing-emotion.

✅ *"Same tutor every class. So she actually builds on what she did last week — not starting from scratch."*
— Repetition for emphasis (tutor continuity + builds on what she did). Conversational connector "so." Anchored in practical observation.

---

## Sample lines that BREAK the voice

❌ *"I was blown away by how confident my son became!"*
— Performed emotion + exclamation point. Real creator says: *"He's started attempting problems he would've skipped before. That's the shift."*

❌ *"Cuemath has completely transformed our family's relationship with mathematics!"*
— Brochure language. Family / relationship / mathematics — none of these are how she actually speaks. And no exclamation points in this register.

❌ *"Whether your child is in K-12 or working on advanced topics, Cuemath has the perfect solution for your family."*
— Standalone explainer scene. Broadcast intro. "K-12" as a phrase — sounds like an LMS landing page.

❌ *"He's now a math whiz!"*
— Banned phrase pattern. Kid-celebratory exclamation isn't her register. She says: *"He's solid at math now. The real test was the AMC 8 — he didn't skip questions."*

❌ *"I highly recommend Cuemath to all parents seeking quality math tutoring."*
— Testimonial-speak. Real creator: *"If your kid is fast at math, this is worth a free trial."*

❌ *"This program has been incredibly beneficial for my children's academic journey."*
— Five red flags in one sentence: incredibly (bleached), beneficial (vague), academic journey (corporate-speak), children (banned — use kids), parents-speaking-in-deck-language.

## Anti-patterns specific to non-NRI Asian-American Mom Creator

1. **NRI rigour register imported.** "There's a layer of math most school curricula don't have time to reach..." — reads constructed to a Chinese-American school-pickup mum. Save for NRI canons.
2. **Education-system vocabulary.** "Curriculum," "academic enrichment," "grade-level standards" — these are school district letters, not school-pickup conversation.
3. **Standalone explainer / broadcast intro.** "Hi mums! Let me tell you about Cuemath" / "Today I'm sharing..." Drop. Content embeds in proof.
4. **Performed emotion.** "Honestly, that confidence? That's everything." Cut. Replace with observed behaviour.
5. **Brand-canon insertions into body.** Stamping the locked close card into the spoken body. The locked card lives on the END FRAME ONLY for influencer; body uses creator's natural close.
6. **MathFit dimensions spoken in body.** "She's built clarity, application, and confidence." Frameworks live on closing card alone, never in spoken VO.
7. **Naming the coach as the source of outcome.** "Coach Aditi prepped 12 students" — marketplace framing. Outcomes attribute to Cuemath.
8. **Inventing creator life facts.** Writing "I didn't want her to be the seventh" when source said 6 kids. Truth is more specific than what you'll fabricate.
9. **Force-fitting em-dashes everywhere.** Em-dashes are a cadence tool, not a stamp. If a sentence flows fine with a period, use a period.
10. **Same connective tissue across 4 creator scripts.** Each creator's connective tissue should differ. Pattern-locked phrases ("Most of what I found was more worksheets, more repetition") across all four scripts = templating, not differentiation.
11. **MD-reviewer-led rewrites.** Reviewer redlines are sharp on tactical catches; structural shifts in their redline can homogenise four scripts toward one register. Check redline against original creator draft for angle integrity.
12. **Logical gaps preserved under "minimum edit."** If a claim isn't set up earlier in the script, the gap is real regardless of who introduced it. Replace, add evidence, or cut.

## The 5-step process (locked from May 11, calibrated May 12)

Every Asian Mom Creator script goes through:

1. **Register sort** — verify this is the right canon for the creator's audience
2. **Pull source brief** — for first-time creators, verify real life facts; for repeat creators, pull last shipped script from `creative_assets.csv`
3. **Internalise register** — read Erden v13 + this canon + creator's own organic posts; develop ear
4. **Draft in her voice** — v(n+1) ≈ v(n) + 5-10% targeted edits for repeats; full draft from chassis for first-timers
5. **Read-aloud test** — speak as her; if any line sounds like a deck, rewrite
