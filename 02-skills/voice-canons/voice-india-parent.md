# Voice canon — India parent

The register for any India-resident parent creative. Tier-1 metro CBSE/ICSE families and Premium IB/IGCSE families both. Vernacular-mixed: English-led with Hindi/regional phrases for emotional anchors.

This is not a checklist. Read it to develop ear. Write from inside the register, then check against this for blind spots.

---

## Who this parent actually is

She is in her mid-30s to mid-40s. Educated professional, often a working mother. Lives in Bangalore, Mumbai, Delhi NCR, Hyderabad, Chennai — Tier-1 metro. Her kid is in Class 4 to Class 8 at a CBSE/ICSE school (mainstream) or at an IB/IGCSE international school (premium). She speaks English at work and in WhatsApp groups; Hindi or her regional language at home with grandparents and at moments of emotional weight.

She has access to a local tuition tutor at ₹300-500 per session. She is paying ₹800 per Cuemath class. She is constantly justifying that 2-3x premium to herself, to her husband, to her own mother. She is not buying personalisation — that word doesn't move her. She is buying pedigree (top 1% tutors, brand) and pedagogy (the method she can see working).

Maths anxiety in her kid is real. Unlike US NRI kids who arrive 1-2 grades ahead, her kid is in the local school system where the maths-anxiety pipeline is well-documented. Her kid says *"I hate maths"* sometimes — and she heard the same thing from her own classmates 25 years ago.

Outcomes matter. Olympiad rank, contest qualification, school topper position. These are the things she will text her sister about. Grade-level performance reports come every term and she reads every line.

She compares Cuemath against BYJU'S, Vedantu, PW (edtech apps) and against Aakash, FIITJEE, Allen (coaching centres). She is NOT primarily comparing against the local home tutor — that's a separate category in her head, used for "homework help" not "real learning."

## Register markers — how the sentences are shaped

**Sentence length.** Mixed. Often short and direct (*"Same teacher every class."*) interleaved with longer reflective sentences. Avoid uniform stamps — neither all-short nor all-sprawl.

**Conversational connectors.** "Actually," "honestly," "kind of," "so" — these are spoken-English rhythm markers that make written copy land as speech. Don't strip them.

**Vernacular at felt moments.** The English-led register switches to Hindi/regional ONLY at emotional anchor lines — usually the kid's-actual-shift moment or the parent's-realisation moment. Not throughout. One vernacular phrase per script is usually the right dose; two if the script is longer.

**Specificity over abstraction.** Names a board (CBSE/ICSE), a chapter (fractions, integers, percentages, mensuration), a contest (IMO, IOQM, school maths olympiad), a teacher first-name. "Concepts" beats "deep understanding." "Topic" beats "module."

**Outcome-led, not parent-emotion-led.** The kid's behavioural shift is the proof. Parent emotion is the second beat, not the first. (This is the India wedge — flipped from the CD v1 default observed-mother frame which works in US but not India.)

**No softeners.** "I think maybe she's improving" doesn't land. India parents say what they mean. "She's better at fractions now" is the right register.

**Italics on the felt word.** Same as global — one italicised emphasis per line, max. Used for the *felt* word, not the loud one.

## Sample lines that land

✅ *"She's only in Class 5 and she's already asking 'why does this rule work?' That's not memorisation. That's thinking."*
— Specific class. Quoted kid speech. Observed shift named. No marketing-speak.

✅ *"With the local tuition sir it was worksheets. With Cuemath, she **explains** the problem to me at dinner."*
— Comparison without bashing. Observed behaviour. Italicised felt word.

✅ *"Coach [Tutor first name] doesn't give him the answer. She waits. He figures it out. Slowly, this changed how he thinks about maths."*
— Method made visible. Patience as proof. Outcome implied.

✅ *"₹800 a class. We were paying ₹500 for the local tutor and getting Class 4 worksheets. The difference is the concepts — she actually understands."*
— Direct value justification. Anchored in concepts (parent word), not personalisation.

✅ *"Mere bête ne pehli baar bola — *'mom, can we do more maths?'* — that was the moment I knew."*
— Vernacular at the felt-moment line. Quoted kid speech as proof. Specific moment.

## Sample lines that BREAK the voice

❌ *"Unlock your child's mathematical potential with personalised learning."*
— Bleached words (unlock, potential, personalised) + abstract verbs + no specificity. Could be any edtech.

❌ *"Cuemath builds a foundation that lasts a lifetime."*
— Generic SaaS copy. "Builds a foundation" is bridging language. India parent says "fundamentals strong ho rahe hain" or "concepts clear hain."

❌ *"Watch your child develop a love for learning."*
— Banned phrase. India parent describes specific observed behaviour, not a developmental arc.

❌ *"Our hand-picked specialists deliver world-class mathematics education."*
— Five bleached/banned words in one sentence. "Specialists" not in vocab. "Hand-picked" + "world-class" both bleached.

❌ *"Your child will feel more confident in math."*
— "math" instead of "maths" (India = maths). "Feel" instead of observed behaviour. "More confident" without anchor.

❌ *"Make learning fun with our gamified platform."*
— "Fun" as lead claim — banned per CD v1. Even for India, fun is SUPPORTING not lead. Try: "Visual problem-solving keeps her engaged — and the concepts stay."

## Anti-patterns specific to India

1. **Parent-emotion-led hooks.** "I worry about my daughter's future" — too parent-centred. India lead is the kid's observed behaviour.
2. **Premium-tier framing without value justification.** Stating ₹800 without anchoring why it's worth it = drop. Always pair with concept-clarity outcome or specific kid shift.
3. **English-only with no vernacular touchpoint.** Loses 30-40% of emotional landing. Use vernacular at one anchor moment per script.
4. **"Boards" / "Class 10" / "Class 12" prep references.** Out of K-8 scope. Cuemath India is K-8.
5. **NRI/diaspora cultural references in India creative.** No "back home" / "settled abroad" framings. India creative is for India-resident parents.
6. **Hand-picked / world-class / unlock** — all bleached, all stale.
7. **Comparing to local tutor as PRIMARY competitor.** Today's frame is BYJU'S / Vedantu / PW + coaching centres, not the local home tutor.
8. **Generic "edtech" framing.** India parent is suspicious of edtech apps after BYJU'S decline. Cuemath positions as tuition / coaching (the category) not edtech.
