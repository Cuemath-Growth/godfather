# Voice canon — US Second-Gen / 1.5-Gen Asian-origin parent

The register for US Second-Gen creative. Indian + East Asian-origin parents who grew up in the US or came as kids. Bay Area-heavy + Frisco/Plano-tech-suburbs + East Coast tech hubs. Evidence-first, Instagram/Reddit/YouTube primary, US-company service standards.

Per the May 12 strategic call: NO "Indian" framing in copy. Global brand voice. This audience is American by expectation — they hold Cuemath to US-company service standards, not Indian-startup standards.

---

## Who this parent actually is

She is 28-42. Grew up in the US (1.5-gen) or born here to immigrant parents (2nd-gen). Holds a degree from a US university — often Stanford, Cal, UT, Georgia Tech, Cornell. Works as a product manager at Microsoft, software engineer at Verizon, architect at Rivian, executive at a B2B SaaS company. Lives in Dublin CA, San Ramon, Tracy, Frisco TX, McKinney, Austin, parts of NJ/NY tri-state, Boston suburbs.

She is Indian / Chinese / Korean / Filipino by heritage, American by everyday expectation. She is fluent in two cultural registers and operates primarily in the American one. She googles before she buys. She compares. She reads Reddit threads. She lurks in r/Parenting and r/educationalsoftware. She holds *every* service to US standards — Apple support quality, Amazon delivery promises, Stripe-tier dashboard usability.

Her primary anxiety is *"Will my child get ahead of their peers?"* — different from first-gen's "fall behind." Her kid is fine. Her kid is in honors track. Her kid scored 95th percentile on the diagnostic. But ahead of peers means top-of-class, RSM-cohort placement, AMC 8 honor roll, AP Calc by junior year, top-tier college. She is competitively oriented, not anxiously oriented.

She is RSM-aware. Mathnasium-aware. Outschool-aware. Bhanzu skeptical-aware. She has probably already trialled one or two competitors before finding Cuemath. She wants evidence-led proof — specific outcomes, specific tutor credentials, specific platform demonstrations.

Her trust mechanism is peer-testimonial + product-evidence. Not community referral (that's first-gen). She'll read Reddit threads. She'll search "cuemath vs RSM" before booking. She'll evaluate the product itself on the trial.

She speaks English exclusively in her professional and parenting life. Her home language exists at the periphery — at her parents' house, at cultural-festival moments, in lullabies she sang her kid. She's bilingual but operates in English. Vernacular phrases in brand copy land as imported, not native.

## Register markers — how the sentences are shaped

**Specific numbers, named outcomes.** Percentile gains, contest placements, course track names, school honor board placements. Vague proof doesn't land. "Improved a lot" reads as no-proof. "80th to 95th in eight months" reads as evidence.

**Comparison fluency.** She has researched the alternatives. Comparison framing lands — but stays one-sided ("here's what we do") not bashing-named-competitor.

**US-fluent register.** "She's killing it," "he's crushing the AMC prep," "we're in honors" — these phrasings sound native. Indian-English idioms ("she's brilliant in maths") sound imported.

**Short, declarative sentences.** Less reflective sprawl than first-gen. More punch. *"Same tutor. Three years. AMC distinguished. Done."*

**Service-standard expectations.** She wants the platform to work cleanly, the schedule to be predictable, the reports to be accurate, the academic counsellor to be responsive. Any operational friction is grounds for churn.

**Evidence over emotion.** First-gen accepts a 60-second story arc. Second-gen wants the score first, the story second. Lead with the number.

**No vernacular in brand copy.** Her audience speaks English at home. Hindi phrases in our copy read as someone-else's-grandmother. Vernacular allowed ONLY at influencer parent-quote moments (e.g. quoting her own mother) and even then sparingly.

## Sample lines that land

✅ *"95th percentile. Eight months. Same coach since the trial. She's done the work."*
— Four short declaratives. Specific number. Time-bound. Coach continuity. Kid as agent ("she's done the work") not the brand.

✅ *"AMC 8 Distinguished. He stayed up after class to ask why the geometry problem worked. That's the part Cuemath built."*
— Specific contest tier. Behavioural detail (stayed up to ask). Brand mentioned in proof beat, not body.

✅ *"We compared. Kumon was worksheets. RSM was group. Cuemath is one tutor who keeps showing up. The third one is what worked for our daughter."*
— Three-comparison structure. Differentiator without bashing. Specific to her daughter.

✅ *"He's been doing the AP Calc track since 8th grade. Same coach throughout. That's the only reason this works — continuity."*
— Specific track. Specific grade start. Continuity named as the mechanism.

✅ *"The platform is actually usable. The tutor reports are detailed. The counsellor calls when she says she will. That's table stakes I shouldn't have to mention — and I shouldn't have to switch."*
— Service-standards register. Three named operational details. Honest admission (shouldn't have to mention).

---

## Sample lines that BREAK the voice

❌ *"Watch your child fall in love with maths!"*
— Wrong audience emotion. Second-gen mum doesn't want falling-in-love-with-maths; she wants outcomes. Plus exclamation point. Plus "maths" instead of "math" for US.

❌ *"Our Indian-trained tutors deliver world-class personalised learning."*
— Three failures: Indian (banned per global pivot), world-class (bleached), personalised-as-USP (doesn't move second-gen anyway).

❌ *"Give your child the gift of mathematical understanding."*
— Gift framing repels second-gen. She's not giving a gift — she's making a placement decision.

❌ *"Loved by thousands of parents like you!"*
— Loved (bleached) + parents-like-you (zero specificity). Replace with named cohort: "Bay Area parents in tech are switching from RSM to Cuemath."

❌ *"Unlock your child's full potential with our expert tutors."*
— Unlock + potential + expert (mixed bleached words). Plus "full potential" is brochure-speak. Second-gen wants a number, not a concept.

❌ *"Make math fun!"*
— Banned phrase, banned punctuation, wrong-audience claim. Her kid being good at maths is the point; "fun" is a side effect, not a value prop.

## Anti-patterns specific to US Second-Gen

1. **Cultural-rigor framing (Indian-touch / Indian-method).** Banned per global pivot. Second-gen specifically reads this as imported. They want US-company quality, not South-Asian-origin nostalgia.
2. **Vernacular phrases in brand copy.** Hindi / Mandarin / Korean phrases land as someone-else's-grandmother. Save for influencer parent-quote moments only.
3. **Community-trust framing as primary proof.** "Three families at our temple" is first-gen proof. Second-gen wants peer-evidence + product-evidence ("Reddit threads," "the platform itself," "specific outcomes").
4. **Vague outcome claims.** "Improved a lot" doesn't move her. "80th to 95th in eight months" does. Specific or skip.
5. **Long-form first-gen-style story arcs.** Second-gen has less patience for the 60-second build. Lead with the result, story is the proof.
6. **Exclamation points + hype words.** "Amazing!" / "Game-changing!" — wrong register entirely. Calm, declarative, specific.
7. **"Catch up" framing.** Her kid isn't behind. She's not buying remediation. Buying acceleration.
8. **Service / ops weakness.** She'll churn over a single missed counsellor call. Operational reliability is part of the brand promise for her.
9. **"Make math fun."** Banned globally; especially repels second-gen. She wants results, not entertainment.
10. **Bay-Area register on Texas-targeted ads.** Texas second-gen is closer to first-gen behavior (more community-trust). Geo-segment the register.
