# Voice canon — UK Settled British-Indian / Asian-origin parent

The register for UK Settled UK Asian-origin family creative. 2nd-3rd generation, UK-fluent, grammar-school-aspirational. Per Naina's earlier audience call — this is the primary UK target (East Asian UK families fold here with light register adjustment; recent UK NRI is a separate cell).

Per the May 12 strategic call: NO "Indian" framing in copy. Global brand voice. Vernacular phrases at parent-emotional-anchor moments allowed (similar logic to US NRI canons).

---

## Who this parent actually is

She is 32-46. Born in the UK or moved here as a young child. Her parents came from Punjab, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Kerala, or East Africa in the 70s-90s. She is UK-cultural by identity, Indian-heritage by family. Speaks English exclusively; understands her family's language but rarely uses it outside her parents' home.

She lives in London (Harrow, Wembley, Slough, Hounslow, Redbridge, Newham, Edgware), West Midlands (Birmingham, Leicester, Coventry), Manchester (Bolton, Stockport), Glasgow (Newton Mearns), or commuter towns around these. Her social density of fellow British-Indians is high. She is in her local mum-WhatsApp group and her school year-group WhatsApp.

Her education-priorities are British. The 11+ is the gateway — Y5-Y6 prep window, sit Sep-Oct of Y6, results Jan. After that, grammar school or selective indie school placement, GCSE excellence, A-Level rigour, Oxbridge or Russell Group aspiration. She knows the system inside-out and is fluent in its specific vocabulary.

She is competitive in the British way — quietly. Doesn't talk about her child's exam prep openly at the school gates (UK norm). Discusses it in the closed mum-WhatsApp and at family weddings. The Indian-heritage academic-rigour register exists *culturally* but the British outward-facing register is understated.

She is grammar-school-aspirational. Or scholarship-aspirational (Westminster, St Paul's, King's, the regional indie schools). She is skeptical of Indian-origin brands ("isn't this just for India parents?") — earn her trust with substance and British-specific competence, not cultural appeal.

She compares Cuemath against Atom Learning (self-serve adaptive, drills, no live tutor) and against Kumon UK / Explore Learning (centre-group format). She is aware of MyTutor (marketplace, rotating-tutor) and Bonas MacFarlane (premium 11+ specialist) but those aren't her primary considerations.

## Register markers — how the sentences are shaped

**British English everywhere.** "maths" (NOT math). "Mum" (NOT mom). "Year 5/6" (NOT Grade 5/6). "Tuition" or "tutor" (both acceptable; "tuition" is the older British term, "tutor" is increasingly common in younger British-Indian register).

**BrE spelling lock.** Personalised · centre · colour · recognise · realise · programme · honour · favourite · neighbour · grey (not gray). On-screen text and copy both.

**Understated register.** Achievement claims land but never as boasts. *"She got into Henrietta Barnett"* — flat-toned, factual. Not *"She crushed the 11+!"* (American register).

**Long-form patience.** UK British-Indian mum will read. Reads the LP fold-by-fold. Reads the FAQ. Reads the testimonial cards in full. Long-form rewards depth.

**School / system fluency.** Specific school names (Henrietta Barnett, Reading Boys, Aylesbury Grammar, Wilson's, Tiffin, Newstead Wood). Specific test components (VR, NVR, English, Maths). Specific exam timings (CEM vs GL Assessment).

**Friend-network / mum-WhatsApp framing.** "Three families in our group are on it." "Recommended by a mum at the school gates." Trust travels through proximity-of-people but in a quieter register than US first-gen (which has temple-community proof).

**Vernacular at felt moments only.** Parent-emotional-anchor moment — kid's behavioral shift, parent's realisation. NOT in brand copy. NOT in body of statics. Reserved for influencer scripts at the single emotional-anchor line per script.

**Skepticism of Indian-origin branding.** She'll trust an Indian-origin company that EARNS it through British-specific competence, not one that LEADS with Indian credentials. Mention IIT or Stanford / MIT tutor backgrounds AFTER she trusts the system, not before.

## Sample lines that land

✅ *"Year 5 to Y8, one Cuemath coach. The 11+ is one chapter — not the whole programme."*
— Year (not Grade). UK-fluent grade label. Longitudinal frame. Anti-specialist positioning. "Programme" (BrE spelling).

✅ *"She's been with her Cuemath tutor for two years. Knew her strengths through Y4. Carried her through the 11+. Still with her now in Y7."*
— Tenure proof. Specific years. Longitudinal narrative. UK-fluent register throughout.

✅ *"My daughter's at Wilson's after the 11+. The maths is a step up — Cuemath helped her settle in faster than I expected."*
— Specific grammar school name. Realistic outcome ("settled in faster," not "topped the class"). Understated register.

✅ *"Three families in our mum-WhatsApp are on Cuemath. One had her son at St Paul's after 11+, another's daughter is at Reading Boys. That's how I found it."*
— Mum-WhatsApp as trust mechanism. Specific schools as proof. British-fluent discovery story.

✅ *"Properly patient. Doesn't rush her. Doesn't give her the answer. That's what's worked for my daughter — she was used to having maths spoon-fed."*
— "Properly patient" (BrE intensifier). Trustpilot-mined word ("patient"). Specific behavioural shift. Self-aware comparison.

## Sample lines that BREAK the voice

❌ *"Help your child master math with personalized tutoring!"*
— Wrong spelling (math instead of maths), exclamation point (off-register for UK British-Indian understated tone), "master" as verb feels boast-y.

❌ *"Our Indian-trained tutors deliver world-class results."*
— "Indian-trained" banned per global pivot. "World-class" bleached. UK British-Indian audience specifically resistant to Indian-credentialing leads.

❌ *"Watch your child fall in love with math!"*
— Banned phrase pattern. Wrong spelling. Exclamation. Falling-in-love framing is American.

❌ *"Get the edge on the 11+ with our 11+ specialist tutors."*
— "11+ specialist" framing banned per school-maths-plus-exam positioning. "Get the edge" — wrong register, sounds American + tabloid.

❌ *"Trusted by hundreds of moms across the UK."*
— "Moms" (use Mums). Generic "hundreds" (no specifics). Trusted (bleached).

❌ *"Your son will absolutely smash the 11+!"*
— Three failures: future-promise (we don't guarantee outcomes), "absolutely smash" (American slang), exclamation point (off-register).

## Anti-patterns specific to UK Settled British-Indian

1. **American register imported.** "Crushed it," "absolutely killed the test," "math whiz" — all wrong. UK British-Indian is understated.
2. **Indian-credentialing leads.** "Indian-trained tutors," "rigour of Indian education." Banned globally + specifically resistant from this audience.
3. **Exclamation points + hype.** Off-register entirely. UK British-Indian register is calm-considered, never breathless.
4. **AmE spelling on UK creative.** "Personalized" instead of "Personalised." "Color" instead of "Colour." "Programme" not "Program." Critical for native-speaker trust signal.
5. **"Mom" instead of "Mum."** Immediate dialect mismatch.
6. **"Grade 5" / "5th grade" instead of "Year 5".** UK education system uses Year. Critical fluency signal.
7. **11+ specialist framing.** Banned per school-maths-plus-exam positioning. *"Not Just An 11+ Tutor"* is the right contrast frame.
8. **GCSE references in K-8 Cuemath copy.** Out of K-8 cap. GCSE is Y10-Y11 territory — handled by Trial Mastery UK for objection-handling, not by Cuemath UK product scope.
9. **Naming competitors.** Atom / MyTutor / Bonas / Kumon — describe by characteristic, never name.
10. **Generic "hundreds of mums" claims.** UK British-Indian wants school-name specificity ("Henrietta Barnett," "Wilson's"). Generic = no trust.
11. **Vernacular in brand copy.** Hindi / Punjabi / Gujarati phrases at influencer-parent-quote moments only. NOT in body of Meta static or LP hero. UK British-Indian audience is UK-cultural — vernacular brand copy reads imported.
12. **Performative cultural celebration.** Diwali ads with mandirs and bhangra — patronising. Cultural moments land when used quietly (e.g. tutor-coach-relationship deepening over Diwali break) not when used as the headline.
