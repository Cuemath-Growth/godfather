# Voice canon — US First-Gen Asian-origin parent

The register for US First-Gen Asian-origin creative. Indian + East Asian (Chinese, Korean, Filipino) families. Came to the US as adults. Texas-heavy + Midwest metros + Bay Area first-immigration enclaves. Community-trust-led, WhatsApp/Facebook/YouTube primary.

Per the May 12 strategic call: NO "Indian" framing in copy. Global brand voice. But vernacular phrases at parent-emotional-anchor moments remain allowed — that's parent voice, not brand positioning.

---

## Who this parent actually is

She is 35-50. Moved to the US in her 20s or early 30s. Works in tech, engineering, medicine, finance — the service-sector knowledge economy that pulled her family here. Lives in Frisco, Plano, Katy, Sugar Land, Houston, Leander (TX) or Fremont, Pleasanton, Union City (Bay Area) — communities where her cultural cohort is dense.

She has lived two decades in the US but operates day-to-day in a cultural-Indian / cultural-Chinese / cultural-Korean register. Temple, gurdwara, church, mosque — these are her social anchors. WhatsApp daily, Facebook 1 hour a day, YouTube for upskilling. Instagram less — her teenage kid has her account but she doesn't post.

Her primary anxiety is *"Will my child fall behind when it matters?"* She is not yet anxious — her kid is doing fine — but she is constantly prepping. The acceleration mindset is baked in. Calculus BC by senior year. AP everything. SAT 1500+. Top STEM major at a top-tier college. This is non-negotiable in her cultural cohort, even when she'd never say it out loud at a PTA meeting.

She trusts personal recommendation from inside her community network — temple parent group, residential community WhatsApp, the colleague's wife who already enrolled her kid. *"Siobhan Bora referred me"* beats any ad headline. Once she trusts you, she stays for years — 3-5+ year commitment is normal. She is price-sensitive at renewal but loyal during enrolment.

She compares Cuemath against Bhanzu (cheaper, familiar, founder-led — but "impressive at first, then thin"), Kumon/Mathnasium (centre-based group, OK for basics not for real depth), and against the local 1:1 home tutor or marketplace (Outschool, Wyzant — fragmented, no continuity).

She speaks English at work, English with her kids, her home language with her own parents and at moments of emotional weight. Hindi / Tamil / Telugu / Mandarin / Korean — depending on her origin. Her kids understand the home language but reply in English. She knows this. She accepts it.

## Register markers — how the sentences are shaped

**Cultural-rigor without the cultural label.** She approaches maths education with the seriousness of someone whose own pathway out of South Asia / East Asia ran through STEM. She doesn't call it "rigour" — she calls it "real maths." Brand voice picks up the seriousness without naming the cultural origin.

**Long-form patience.** She is happy to read a 60-second video, a 4-fold LP, a 90-character description. She is researching. Speed bias is for the second-gen. First-gen will read.

**Community-trust references.** "Three families at our temple," "the kids on our street," "my colleague's daughter." Trust travels through proximity-of-people, not proximity-of-evidence.

**Multi-year mindset.** She talks in years, not months. "We've been with [tutor] for two years." "He started in 5th grade." The continuity claim lands hardest here because she IS the multi-year customer.

**English-language register.** Indian-English-inflected (for Indian-origin) or American-English with home-language emotional moments (for East Asian). Avoid pure American slang ("totally crushed it," "killed it") — feels off-register.

**Outcomes she values:** Test scores (AMC honor, SAT subscore, AP grade). Course placement (honors algebra, AP Calc track). Cultural-cohort markers (Sciences Olympiad, MathCounts state). Long-term — *will this kid get into a top STEM major?*

## Sample lines that land

✅ *"Same tutor. Three years. He's now solving problems we couldn't solve at his age. That's what I wanted from this."*
— Tenure (3 years). Specific outcome (problems beyond his age). Parent's own admission (we couldn't solve them). Indian/Asian parent honesty register.

✅ *"He didn't just pass the AMC 8. He stayed up after to ask his coach why one problem worked. That's the part that matters."*
— Specific contest. Behavioural shift (curiosity beyond the test). Coach mentioned by role not name.

✅ *"At our temple, three families are already using Cuemath. That's how we found it."*
— Community-trust origin story. Specific count. Temple as the trust anchor.

✅ *"The first year was slow. He had to relearn how to think about word problems. Year two, you could see it click. We're starting year three."*
— Multi-year narrative. Honest about the slow start. Specific cognitive shift (word problems). Long-tail commitment.

✅ *"At dinner she explained the Pythagorean theorem to her younger brother. Used a triangle on the napkin. I wasn't asking. She just did it."*
— Specific moment. Quoted/observed behaviour. Younger sibling as the audience. No marketing-speak.

## Sample lines that BREAK the voice

❌ *"Give your child the gift of personalised math tutoring."*
— Bleached words (gift, personalised). First-gen parent isn't gifting math — she's investing in long-term cohort positioning. "Gift" is for birthday cards.

❌ *"Our hand-picked Indian tutors bring the rigour of Indian mathematics education."*
— Three failures: hand-picked (bleached), Indian (banned per global pivot), rigour-of-Indian-education (cultural framing). All retired.

❌ *"Unlock your child's STEM future today."*
— Generic "unlock" + "today" creates fake-urgency + STEM-future is brochure-speak. First-gen parent thinks in years, not "today."

❌ *"Watch your child fall in love with math!"*
— Banned phrase pattern ("fall in love with X" + exclamation point). She's not raising her kid to love maths; she's raising her kid to be good at maths. Different motivation.

❌ *"Catch up on grade-level math with one-on-one help."*
— Remediation framing. First-gen audience is acceleration-mode, not remediation. "Catch up" repels her — her kid is supposed to be ahead.

❌ *"Trusted by parents in your community!"*
— Three failures: trusted (bleached on premium), generic "your community" (no specificity), exclamation point. Replace with named community: "Three families at the Frisco BAPS temple." Specific = trusted.

## Anti-patterns specific to US First-Gen

1. **Indian-cultural framing as a SELLING POINT.** Retired per May 12. "Indian tutors / Indian-trained / Indian rigour" all out. Parents may still talk this way; brand doesn't echo it.
2. **Speed / urgency framing.** First-gen reads. Reads everything. Doesn't respond to "limited spots" or "act now."
3. **Excitement / exclamation register.** Calm-considered. Never breathless.
4. **Generic "community" without specifics.** "Trusted by parents in your community" = nothing. Named community ("at the Frisco BAPS temple") = something.
5. **Single-grade fixation.** First-gen parent thinks 3rd-grade-through-12th. Don't pitch "5th grade math tutoring" — pitch "the program that gets her to AP Calc."
6. **Bay-Area register on Texas-targeted ads.** First-gen Texas is community-trust-led; first-gen Bay Area is more evidence-led (closer to second-gen). Geo-segment.
7. **Discount-led framing.** "20% off first month!" — value-erosion. First-gen pays full price gladly; price-sensitivity surfaces at renewal, not enrolment.
8. **Talking to her — not at her child.** "Learn math!" addressed to the kid — wrong audience. She's the decision maker.
