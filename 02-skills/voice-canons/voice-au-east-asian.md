# Voice canon — East Asian Australian parent

The register for East Asian Australian creative. Chinese, Korean, Vietnamese, Filipino, Taiwanese Australian families. Pure AU-fluent — no vernacular phrases in brand copy or even at parent-emotional-anchor moments (this audience's home language stays at home; copy reads imported the moment it surfaces in marketing).

Same audience as Indian-Australian on exam-prep priorities (OC, Selective, Scholarship, NAPLAN) but a different cultural-register surface. Tighter overall constraint than the diaspora-Indian canon.

---

## Who this parent actually is

She is 30-45. Born in Australia or came as a young child. Chinese (often Cantonese-speaking Hong Kong heritage in Sydney, or Mandarin-speaking from Mainland / Taiwan / Singapore), Korean, Vietnamese, Filipino, Taiwanese. Her family's first immigration wave was post-1975 for Vietnamese, 1990s-2000s for mainland Chinese, 1980s-onwards for Korean.

She lives in Sydney (Eastwood, Carlingford, Beecroft, Hornsby, Chatswood — Chinese; Strathfield, Eastwood — Korean; Cabramatta, Bankstown — Vietnamese), Melbourne (Box Hill, Glen Waverley, Mount Waverley, Doncaster — Chinese; Springvale — Vietnamese), Brisbane (Sunnybank), Perth (Willetton), Adelaide (Salisbury).

She speaks English at work, English with her kids. Her home language exists at her parents' / grandparents' house and at family events. Her kids understand some but reply mostly in English. She is bilingual but operates day-to-day in English. Unlike the UK British-Indian / Indian-Australian audiences, where vernacular emotional anchors land in influencer scripts, East Asian Australian copy reads cleaner when vernacular stays out.

Her primary anxiety mirrors Indian-Australian: OC Test (Y4), Selective (Y6), Scholarship (Y5-6 ACER/Edutest). NAPLAN baseline. Often with intensified academic-rigour expectations — the East Asian Australian cohort has the highest selective school placement rates in NSW alongside Indian-Australian families.

She is community-aware in her own cohort — local Chinese mum groups, Korean church communities, Vietnamese parent-school WhatsApp. Trust travels through her ethnic-language WeChat / KakaoTalk / Viber groups in addition to English-language mum-WhatsApp.

She compares Cuemath against Kumon (heavy AU presence), Cluey Learning (AU-native online 1:1, rotating tutors), and against the local 1:1 tutor (often a university-student tutor in the East Asian-density suburbs).

## How she actually speaks — verbatim phrases

Limited direct-quote density for this audience in current Trustpilot corpus. The voice patterns drawn from observed AU East Asian parent forums (Whirlpool, ParentZone, school-specific WhatsApp transcripts), AU East Asian parent education-talk research, and the Asian-American Mom Creator canon (which has similar register patterns).

**On her kid:**
- *"He's in Year 4. The OC Test is next year — we want to see if he can sit Selective in Year 6 after that."*

- *"My daughter's at James Ruse after the Selective. We knew Cuemath was working when she settled into the maths there."*

**On Cuemath:**

- *"We tried Cluey. Different tutor each session. Couldn't build anything."*

- *"We did Kumon for two years. Worksheets. He memorised. Switched to Cuemath. Different shape."*

**On the system:**
- *"OC and Selective are both in May. NAPLAN is March. They have to peak through the whole term."*
- *"James Ruse is the target — or one of the other top selectives. We're working toward it from Year 4."*

## Register markers — how the sentences are shaped

**Pure AU-fluent English.** No vernacular. No Cantonese / Mandarin / Korean / Vietnamese in brand copy or influencer body. Save home-language phrases for the rare cases of authentic parent-quote use, and even then sparingly.

**BrE spelling.** Personalised · centre · colour · recognise · programme · maths · Mum · favourite.

**Sentence cadence: short + factual.** Less reflective sprawl than Indian-Australian or UK British-Indian canons. More declarative. *"Same teacher every class. Two years now. She's in honors algebra."*

**Specific outcomes.** Selective school name. NAPLAN band. Percentile. Course track. Generic "improved" reads as no-proof.

**Service-standards register.** East Asian Australian parents (especially second-gen) hold operational reliability to high standards. The platform, the reports, the counsellor call timing — all matter. Service friction is grounds for churn.

**Community-trust framing but in own cohort.** "A family from our Chinese church," "A mum from the Korean school WhatsApp," "Three families from our Eastwood mum group." More specific than Indian-Australian generic community references because East Asian Australian communities cluster tighter geographically.

**No exclamation points + no hype.** Same calm-considered register as other AU + UK canons. Achievement claims land as flat-toned statements of fact.

**Outcome-led specificity.** Number first, story second. "Year 4 → OC Test passed. Same teacher into Year 5" lands. "She's grown so much!" doesn't.

## Sample lines that land

✅ *"Year 4. OC Test passed. Same teacher into Year 5 prep for Selective."*
— Four declaratives. Specific AU test names. Continuity proof.

✅ *"James Ruse after the Selective. She settled in faster than I expected — the maths there is a different level."*
— Specific selective school. Realistic outcome. Honest acknowledgment of difficulty.

✅ *"Two years with the same teacher. She knows my son's pattern — he panics on time, his maths is solid. Work this term is on the panic, not the maths."*
— Tenure proof. Specific cognitive observation. Brand approach implied.

✅ *"Cluey rotated tutors. Cuemath doesn't. That's the actual difference."*
— Short comparison. No competitor-bashing. Specific differentiator.

✅ *"Three families from our Eastwood mum group are on Cuemath. One had her daughter at North Sydney Girls. That's how we found it."*
— Specific geographic community. Named selective school. Trust-network origin.

## Sample lines that BREAK the voice

❌ *"Help your child crush the OC Test!"*
— "Crush" wrong (American slang). Exclamation point off-register. Future-promise framing.

❌ *"我们的优秀老师为您的孩子提供个性化数学辅导!"*
— Chinese-language copy. Even if your audience is Chinese-Australian, your CREATIVE stays English. Vernacular in brand body reads imported. (Note: this is different from running Chinese-language ads on WeChat — those are operationally different from English Meta ads.)

❌ *"Make math fun with our gamified platform!"*
— Banned phrase ("fun" as lead), banned spelling ("math" vs "maths"), exclamation point.

❌ *"Watch your child fall in love with maths."*
— Falling-in-love framing repels this audience. They're not buying maths-affection; they're buying placement.

❌ *"Our hand-picked Asian tutors deliver world-class results."*
— Hand-picked (bleached), "Asian tutors" (cultural-credentialing — banned), world-class (bleached), guaranteed-outcome implication.

❌ *"Trusted by hundreds of families across Australia."*
— Generic "hundreds" (no specifics), trusted (bleached). Replace: "Families from the Eastwood / Strathfield / Sunnybank mum groups."

### LP hero
- *"Year 3 to Year 8. One teacher. The Selective is one chapter."*
- *"Maths for Year 5 and Year 6. Same teacher. School maths + Selective in one programme."*

### Influencer (East Asian Australian mum creator)

> *"Three families from our Eastwood mum group were on Cuemath. One had her daughter at North Sydney Girls after the Selective. I was looking for OC prep for my Year 4 — that's how I found it."*

## Anti-patterns specific to East Asian Australian

1. **Vernacular in brand copy.** Mandarin / Cantonese / Korean / Vietnamese phrases in body or hero. Reads imported. Save for WeChat-channel-specific creative ONLY (separate operational format, not in scope of this canon).
2. **American register imported.** Same as Indian-Australian — "crush," "killed it," exclamation points off-register.
3. **Cultural-credentialing leads.** "Asian tutors," "Asian-style rigour" — wrong register. East Asian Australian audience is Australian by identity, not Asian-credentialing-seeking.
4. **UK / US system vocabulary.** "11+" / "Grade 5" / "AP Calc" — wrong country.
5. **NAPLAN out of season.** Same lock as Indian-Australian.
6. **Performative cultural celebration.** Lunar New Year ads with dragon dancers + tutor headshots — patronising. Cultural moments land quietly (tutor-coach-continuity through Lunar New Year break, school-pickup-mum-group conversation) not as headline.
7. **Generic "hundreds" claims.** East Asian Australian wants suburb-specific community references ("Eastwood mum group," "Strathfield Korean church," "Sunnybank Chinese parent network").
8. **Exclamation points + hype.** Off-register.
9. **AmE spelling.** "Personalized" wrong; should be "Personalised."
10. **"Grade" / "Mom".** Use Year / Mum.
11. **Naming competitors.** Cluey / Kumon — describe by characteristic, never name.
