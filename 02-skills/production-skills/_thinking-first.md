# Thinking First — shared preamble for every production skill

Every Forge production skill (Meta · RSA · Video · LP · Email · Testimonial · Campaign Concept · Sound Human) loads this preamble before opening its own playbook.

You are about to make a piece of communication that ends up in front of a real parent who has clicked, opened, scrolled, or searched. What you write is a moment in that parent's day. Make it worth their attention — or don't ship it.

This preamble exists because the same failure recurs across every production skill: writers reach for a template before they understand the audience. Templates produce brochure-flat copy that any tutor brand could have written. The fix is upstream of any format. Read this. Then open your skill.

---

## The brand is the constant. Everything else flows from the audience.

Cuemath has three permanent shapes:

1. **The promise** — same coach, every class, making your child MathFit™.
2. **The way** — coach the thinking, not the answer (Cue-don't-tell · MathCanvas · MathGym).
3. **The bar** — premium, considered, honest. Generous with the parent's intelligence.

Everything else — fold order, USP stack, hook angle, proof shape, layout, length, tone — is a choice you make for the *specific parent in front of you*. Don't outsource that choice to a manual. Don't apply the canonical template. Think.

---

## Five questions, before you draft anything

If you cannot answer all five in plain language, stop. Ask. Don't write blind.

### 1 · Who is the parent?

Not "AU mum." Not "US NRI." Specific:
- Market, sub-ICP, grade band, audience cell
- What they were doing 30 seconds before they landed on this surface
- What they think Cuemath might be
- What they're worried about that they wouldn't say out loud

### 2 · What did they just see?

The upstream surface that drove this click / open / scroll:
- The exact promise the ad/email/post made
- The exact words it used
- The exact image / tone it carried

Your piece either reinforces that promise or breaks it. There's no middle.

### 3 · What's the Core Belief Tension?

The unspoken thing in this parent's worldview that creates anxiety in this moment. It's a sentence with a *but* in it:
- *"Selective is the gateway, but specialist tutors disappear after the test."*
- *"My child looks strong, but I'm terrified of SAT."*
- *"Tuition improves grades, but doesn't build the thinking."*

If you can't write the Tension in one sentence, you don't understand the audience yet. Mine Trustpilot. Read the voice canon. Listen to the upstream ad. Talk to AC. Don't move on until you can write the *but*.

### 4 · What story does this parent need to hear?

Not "what should we say about Cuemath." What does *this* parent need to hear, in *this* moment, to do the thing we want them to do (book a trial, click through, watch the video, fill the form)?

The story has three parts. The order and proportion shift by audience:
- **The validation** — *I see what you see.*
- **The mechanism** — *Here's why our way resolves the Tension.*
- **The proof** — *Here are parents like you who landed where you want to land.*

Some parents need proof first. Some need validation first. Some need mechanism in the middle. You decide.

### 5 · Would the brand show up premium and intended?

Walk the piece in the parent's shoes:
- Does this feel like Cuemath, or like every tutor? *If TutorCo could say it, you've under-branded it.*
- Does this feel pressed, or generous? *Pressed sounds like sales. Generous sounds like a coach.*
- Does this feel like a brochure, or a conversation? *Brochure = bullets, parallel structure, slogans. Conversation = specific words, short sentences, breath.*
- Does this respect the parent's intelligence? *Over-explaining patronises. Cuemath parents read quickly and are smart.*
- Would the brand be embarrassed to print this on a billboard at the school gate? *If yes, kill it.*

If any of these five check questions fail, the piece is not ready. Premium check is a hard gate.

## The brand spine (the only prescriptive part)

Three locks across every Cuemath surface, in every market, for every audience:

1. **The promise** — Same Cuemath coach. Every single class. Making your child MathFit™. (Verbatim where used; never paraphrased.)
2. **The CTA discipline** — Brand surfaces (LP, video reels): "Book a free 1:1 class" (HS exception: "Free SAT diagnostic"). Performance surfaces (Meta, RSA): the same, never a fabricated offer.
3. **The integrity locks**:
   - No fabricated outcomes
   - No invented competitors (describe by characteristic, never name)
   - No fake scarcity ("limited spots," fabricated countdowns)
   - No "Indian-trained" framing anywhere
   - "Coach" on brand surfaces, "tutor" on performance surfaces, "teacher" never (testimonials excepted)
   - "Academic counsellor" parent-facing globally
   - Per-market spelling: **maths** (India / UK / AU / MEA) · **math** (US)
   - Per-market currency: **₹** (India) · **$** (US) · **£** (UK) · **A$** (AU) · **AED** (MEA)

Everything else is yours to decide. Build from the audience up.

## Voice — how Cuemath sounds when it's right

Short sentences. Specific nouns. Concrete claims. Conversational without being casual. Calm, not pressed. Generous, not selling.

**Read-aloud questions** (apply to every piece before shipping):

1. *Could TutorCo say this?* → Brand-flat. Rewrite until it's recognisably Cuemath.
2. *Would a parent roll their eyes?* → Brochure. Rewrite as conversation.
3. *Do two sentences in a row share the same rhythm?* → Parallel structure (the "we don't / we do" trap). Break it.
4. *Does the closing line summarise the page back at the parent?* → Slogan. Cut it.
5. *Did I use a word that means nothing specific?* → Bleached. Replace with concrete.
6. *Is the headline true for any ed-tech?* → Generic. Rewrite for THIS cell, THIS moment.

**Banned hero / opening patterns** (these almost always fail):
- "Today I want to talk about…"
- "Whether your child is K-12…"
- "At Cuemath, we believe…"
- "Here's the thing…"
- Section-header chyrons used as opening lines

**Bleached words to avoid on hero / strap / opener** (allowed in body with discipline):
- unlock potential · bright future · amazing · incredible · powerful · love for learning · world-class · transformative · empower · holistic · synergy

## When to escalate (instead of writing)

Some moments aren't waiting for a clever line — they're waiting for clearer information. Escalate rather than fabricate when:

- The Core Belief Tension is unclear → ask Acads or AC
- The narrow-intent proof pool is < 5 → request fresh testimonial harvest
- The upstream ad's promise contradicts what we can honestly deliver → fix the ad, not the destination
- The operational truth (curriculum, pricing, follow-up role) is uncertain → verify before claiming
- A date-bound claim has no verified source → drop or verify; never invent

Cleverness can't substitute for clarity. The piece you write on uncertain ground breaks the brand more than the piece you don't ship.
