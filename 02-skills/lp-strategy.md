# LP Strategy — Cuemath Landing Pages

A thinking framework, not a template.

This skill makes you stop, read the brief, understand the parent in front of you, and *derive* the landing page from what the audience needs to hear — not from a fold order you copied last time.

If you find yourself reaching for "the canonical 9-fold" or "the AU template" before you've read the brief, you're using this skill wrong. Re-read this opener.

---

## What this skill won't give you

- A fold order to copy
- A USP stack to paste
- A design template to fill
- A "right answer" for any audience

What it *will* give you is a way of thinking that lets you derive all four of those for the specific parent landing on this specific page, after clicking this specific upstream creative, in this specific moment.

The brand is the constant. Everything else is a variable. Structure follows story; design follows structure; copy follows design. Always.

---

## The brand spine (the only prescriptive part)

Three things are non-negotiable across every Cuemath LP, in every market, for every audience. Everything else is yours to decide:

1. **The promise** — Same Cuemath coach. Every single class. Making your child MathFit™. (Close fletch verbatim.)
2. **The CTA** — "Book a free 1:1 class." HS-only exception: "Free SAT diagnostic" (CD v1 §5).
3. **The integrity locks** — No fabricated outcomes. No invented competitors. No fake scarcity. No "Indian-trained" framing. No named-competitor disses. "Coach" on brand surfaces, "tutor" on perf surfaces, "teacher" never. "Academic counsellor" parent-facing. Use the spelling and currency of the market.

Everything else — the order of the page, what's in each fold, how many testimonials, where the comparison sits, whether there *is* a comparison — flows from the audience and the story, not from the manual.

## The five-step think

Run these in order. Skipping a step is what produces the LPs that read like brochures.

### Step 1 · Read the brief end-to-end

Before you open a Notion page or a Storybook file:

- **Who is the parent?** Market, sub-ICP, grade band, audience cell. Not "AU" — "AU Indian-Australian, Year 4 mum, NSW, has heard about OC from her sister's kid."
- **What did they click?** The upstream ad — Meta static / RSA / Reel. Pull it. Note the exact promise it made.
- **What's the channel?** Google Search (intent-hot, anxiety-led), Meta (lower intent, scroll-tolerant, emotional warmup possible), LinkedIn (premium, evidence-led), WhatsApp/community (warm referral, trust-already-high), TikTok (vertical-native, hook continuity), Email (warmest, post-form-fill).
- **What's the moment?** A specific seasonal hook (OC test 30 days out) is a different page from an evergreen cold visit.
- **What's the goal?** Trial booking is the default. SAT diagnostic for US HS. Strategy call for warm Meta. Match the offer to the audience's warmth.

If any of these is hazy, stop and ask. Do not write into haze.

### Step 2 · Find the Core Belief Tension

This is the unspoken thing in the parent's worldview that creates anxiety in this moment. It's not a pain point ("my child struggles") — it's a tension between two beliefs the parent holds.

How to find it:
- **Mine Trustpilot.** What do five-star reviewers from this cell say in *their own words* about what changed? The hero-proof signal is here. (60.8% of 5-star reviews mention the tutor as hero, source: 2026-Q1 analysis of 7,918 5-star reviews — but the *texture* of how they describe the tutor varies by cell.)
- **Read the parent voice file.** `02-skills/voice-canons/voice-{cell}.md` — the parent's vocabulary, what they don't say, what makes them flinch.
- **Listen to the upstream ad.** What is the ad *implying* the parent already believes? The LP has to honor that belief or contradict it explicitly.
- **Talk to AC / Acads.** If there's a recurring objection in trial calls for this cell, it's the tension.

A Core Belief Tension is a sentence with a *but* in it. Examples (not templates — illustrations):
- *"Selective is the gateway to opportunity, but specialist tutors disappear after the test."*
- *"My child looks strong in school, but I'm terrified of SAT when high school hits."*
- *"Tuition improves grades, but doesn't build the thinking that wins IIT."*

Write the Tension down before you draft anything. If you can't articulate it in one sentence, you don't understand the audience yet.

### Step 3 · Decide the story

Given the Tension, what story does this parent need to hear to fill the form?

The story has three parts. The proportions and order shift by audience:

- **The validation** — *I see what you see.* Acknowledge the Tension in the parent's own language.
- **The mechanism** — *Here's why our way resolves the Tension.* Method, tutor, continuity, whatever the differentiator is for this cell.
- **The proof** — *Here are parents like you who landed where you want to land.* Testimonials, outcomes, named kids.

Some audiences need proof FIRST and mechanism second (Selective Chasers, EA acceleration parents). Some need validation FIRST and proof later (NRI K-8 first-gen, AU Anglo-Australian). Some need the mechanism explained before they trust the proof (India K-8 metro, US HS). You decide which order serves THIS parent.

### Step 4 · Sequence the folds

Now — and only now — pick the folds.

A fold is a *story beat*, not a content type. "Hero" is not a fold; the *opening beat* is a fold. "Testimonials" is not a fold; the *proof beat* is a fold, which may render as testimonials, or as named-kid outcomes, or as a Trustpilot star count, depending on what's strongest for this cell.

Common beats in a Cuemath LP (pick what serves the story; skip what doesn't):

| Beat | What it does | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Opening** | Confirms the click within 5 seconds | Always |
| **Validation** | Mirrors the Tension in the parent's words | When audience needs to feel seen first |
| **Mechanism** | Shows how Cuemath resolves the Tension | When method is the differentiator |
| **Tutor** | Names the human who'll teach the child | When tutor-continuity is the wedge |
| **Proof** | Specific parents, specific outcomes | Always — placement varies |
| **Comparison** | Sets Cuemath against alternatives | When audience is mid-consideration (MOFU) |
| **Curriculum / scope** | Confirms boards and exam-prep coverage | When operational fit is in question |
| **Operational FAQ** | Removes friction-objections | Always — placement varies |
| **Form** | The conversion | Always — last beat |
| **Close fletch** | Brand sign-off, locked verbatim | Always — final |

You can omit beats. You can repeat beats. You can collapse two beats into one fold. You decide what the audience needs.

The 9-fold canonical pattern is one valid sequence — not the right answer. Use it when it serves the story, ignore it when it doesn't.

### Step 5 · The premium check

Before you finalize, walk the page in the audience's shoes and ask:

1. **Does this feel like Cuemath, or like every tutor?** If you can swap our name for "TutorCo" and the page still works, the brand isn't showing up. Premium brands sound like themselves on every line.
2. **Does this feel pressed, or generous?** Pressed sounds like sales. Generous sounds like a thoughtful coach. Cuemath is a coach.
3. **Does this feel like a brochure, or a conversation?** Headers in title case, parallel "we don't / we do" sentences, slogan-y closes — all brochure. Re-write as a parent talking to a parent.
4. **Does this respect the parent's intelligence?** If the page over-explains, hedges, repeats USPs, or oversells — it patronises. The Cuemath parent reads quickly and is smart.
5. **Would the brand be embarrassed to print this on a billboard at the school gate?** If yes, kill it.

If any of these five check questions fail, the LP isn't ready. The premium check is a hard gate, not a soft preference.

## How to think about each audience

Below are *worked examples* of the five-step think applied to specific cells. They're not templates. They are *how I'd think it through* for these cells — useful as a starting reference, but for your specific LP, do the five-step think yourself.

Where a cell has a stable Core Belief Tension that recurs across LPs (selective audiences are recognizably similar across markets, for example), this section captures the pattern. Where the Tension shifts by intent (an AU Indian-Australian parent on a Selective LP has a different Tension than the same parent on a NAPLAN LP), the cell flags that.

### AU Indian-Australian K-8

**Core Belief Tension (Selective intent):** *"Selective is the gateway to opportunity, but specialist coaching centres rotate tutors every term and disappear after the test."*

**What the brief should tell you:** Year band (Year 3-4 = OC chase; Year 5-6 = Selective chase; Year 6-8 = scholarship chase). Specific test target if known. State (NSW dominates; VIC for SEAL; QLD for Selective Entry). Upstream creative (often parent-testimonial Meta reels).

**What the story likely is for this cell:** Proof first (other Indian-Australian families won OC/Selective), then mechanism (one coach for years, not a sprint), then comparison (vs the coaching-centre alternative they're already considering).

**Beats that work:** Opening → Proof → Tutor-continuity → Mechanism → Comparison → Operational FAQ → Form → Close. Validation often happens *inside* the opening (the headline mirrors the Tension).

**Watch-outs specific to this cell:** Real AU kids in images = banned (Naina May 13 visual lock). Don't position as "Selective specialist" — the wedge IS that we're not specialists. Vernacular phrases OK at emotional anchor moments only.

**Trustpilot signal:** Only ~2 reviews in the 7,918 5-star pool explicitly mention Selective. The pool for this intent is thin — escalate to AU Acads for fresh harvest before relying on Trustpilot alone.

**Voice canon:** `voice-au-indian.md`.

### AU East Asian K-8 (Chinese / Korean / Vietnamese / Filipino)

**Core Belief Tension (Selective intent):** *"Australian schools don't pressure enough, but the existing 'Asian tutoring' centres are mother-tongue or pure drill."*

**What the story likely is:** Peer-validation (other EA families switched), acceleration-specific evidence (1-2 grade levels typical), English-medium delivery as the wedge against mother-tongue centres.

**Watch-outs:** No group-classroom imagery. No Indian-method framing. The audience is EA-specific — keep cultural cues neutral / AU-fluent.

**Voice canon:** `voice-au-east-asian.md`.

### AU Anglo-Australian K-8

**Core Belief Tension (Selective intent):** *"Selective is becoming impossible to ignore, but the culture around it feels foreign and stressful."*

**What the story likely is:** Sustainability throughout — selective excellence WITHOUT the burnout, school maths protected, post-test continuity, calm-and-patient register.

**Watch-outs:** Don't pull from migrant aspiration vocabulary. "Mum" not "mom." Avoid the "Asian mom" cultural-pressure beats — they alienate this cell.

**Voice canon:** `voice-au-anglo.md` *(to be created — Stage 4)*.

### US NRI K-8

**Core Belief Tension:** *"My child looks strong in school, but I'm terrified of SAT/ACT when high school hits."*

**What the story likely is:** Foundation-rebuild framing — strong grades aren't enough, the foundation has hidden gaps, Cuemath finds and fixes them before they collapse at standardized tests.

**Watch-outs:** "Indian-trained tutors" banned everywhere. Cultural-pressure framing alienates Second-gen. Distinguish First-gen TX-heavy (community-trust) from Second-gen Bay-heavy (evidence-first) — they're different sub-cells.

**Voice canons:** `voice-us-first-gen.md` or `voice-us-second-gen.md`.

### US East Asian K-8

**Core Belief Tension:** *"Other kids are ahead, my child needs to stay ahead, but group tutoring centres leave gaps that surface at the gifted-program test."*

**What the story likely is:** Peer-validation + acceleration evidence + personalised diagnostic. EA parents trust evidence more than philosophy. Outcomes-led.

**Voice canon:** `voice-asian-mom-creator.md` (influencer-handoff) or `voice-us-second-gen.md`.

### US High School (NRI and EA, K-12 only US market)

**Core Belief Tension (NRI on Google Search):** *"My sophomore is getting B+s but will bomb SAT because the foundation is shaky."*

**Core Belief Tension (EA on Meta):** *"My child is in advanced math but stressed — competitive but at risk of burnout."*

**What the story likely is (NRI Google):** Anxiety-relief opener, problem-agitation, tutor credibility, diagnostic-first method, score-lift outcomes. Search-intent matched throughout. Google Quality Score discipline.

**What the story likely is (EA Meta):** Warmer, slower build, emotional trust before outcomes, "sustainable excellence" framing.

**Watch-outs:** US HS uses FUAR vocabulary in body (per CD v1 §3). CTA exception applies — "Free SAT diagnostic" is allowed in place of "Book a free 1:1 class."

**Voice canons:** `voice-us-first-gen.md`, `voice-us-second-gen.md`, or `voice-asian-mom-creator.md` depending on cell.

### India K-8 (TOFU brand LP)

**Core Belief Tension:** *"Tuition improves grades, but doesn't build the thinking that wins IIT/NEET/JEE."*

**What the story likely is:** Pedigree + pedagogy lead — IIT/NIT-trained coaches, MathFit method, longitudinal arc to competitive exams. Outcomes named (board topper, Olympiad rank). Vernacular at emotional moments.

**Watch-outs:** "Personalised" allowed in body but not as hero lead. WhatsApp number is the form's primary contact channel, not email. ₹ pricing + 18% GST disclosed.

**Voice canon:** `voice-india-parent.md`.

### India K-8 (LinkedIn premium LP)

**Core Belief Tension:** *"I pay premium school fees, but tuition still feels like an assembly line."*

**What the story likely is:** Premium-tier framing — IIT/NIT-trained coach, transparent ₹800/class pricing, 1:1 (not classroom), pedigree-led from the hero. Pricing fold appears early (BOFU framing).

**Voice canon:** `voice-india-parent.md` (premium register variant).

### UK Settled UK Asian K-8 (11+ / KS2 SATs)

**Core Belief Tension (11+ intent):** *"11+ is everything, but 11+ specialist tutors disappear after the exam and we want continuity through GCSE."*

**What the story likely is:** "Not just an 11+ tutor — Year X to GCSE, one coach, 11+ is one chapter." School maths + 11+ in one plan as the wedge.

**Watch-outs:** GCSE / A-Level out of K-8 cap (mentioned but not centered). UK-system fluency from the hero. **maths** + **mum** + **Year X**.

**Voice canon:** `voice-uk-british-indian.md`.

### UK Anglo-British K-8

**Core Belief Tension:** *"Indie school scholarship / 11+ is creeping in, but the surrounding culture feels like a hot-house migrant arms race."*

**What the story likely is:** Excellence-without-pressure framing, year-round maths quality, indie scholarship as one optional chapter.

**Voice canon:** `voice-uk-anglo.md` *(to be created — Stage 4)*.

### MEA International Expat K-8

**Core Belief Tension:** *"Curriculum continuity across moves matters more than locale, but local tutoring options are thin and the international school is pricey enough already."*

**What the story likely is:** IB/Cambridge curriculum fluency, online 1:1 (works across moves), international register without cultural cues, transparent AED pricing.

**Voice canon:** `voice-mea-expat.md`.

## Sourcing rules (the few things every cell needs)

### Proof — sourced before written

Testimonials must be Trustpilot-verified, gold-standard format, sub-ICP-distributed for the market, and narrow-intent-filtered for single-moment LPs.

- **Min:** 5 cards. If the narrow-intent filter yields < 5, escalate to per-market Acads / customer ops for fresh harvest. Do not soften the filter.
- **Distribution per market** — AU ~70/30 NRI/non-NRI · UK ~60/40 Asian/Anglo · US per cell · India pure Indian · MEA international expat mix.
- **Cross-intent contamination ban** — a Selective LP doesn't carry a NAPLAN testimonial even if both are AU. The page is about one moment.

### Tutor — named, real, not invented

Tutor profile cards use real Cuemath coaches with Acads sign-off. Photo, name, state (or city), years coaching, track record relevant to this LP's intent. Never write a tutor quote you didn't pull from a real interview or testimonial.

### Outcomes — verifiable, named, specific

Named child + grade + outcome. No fabricated percentages. No "moves kids 2 grades ahead" without a verifiable observable. Reframe as "many EA Cuemath students have moved 1-2 grade levels — observed in standardized placements" not "guaranteed 2-grade leap."

### Compare — characteristic, never named

Describe competitor categories by characteristic. "Group coaching centres of 15-25 kids with rotating instructors" — never "James An" or "Kumon." Per format-manual MOFU rule.

### Pricing — operational, market-specific

Pull from `market-operational-models.md`. Never invent a price. Currency must match market (₹ for India, A$ for AU, £ for UK, $ for US, AED for MEA). 18% GST disclosed in India.

### Curriculum / exam-prep — per-market truth

Pull from `market-operational-models.md`. Don't list NAPLAN on a US LP, CBSE on a UK LP, or NCEA on an AU LP. IGCSE / Cambridge always a single dropdown entry.

## Voice — principles, not lists

Cuemath sounds like a thoughtful coach talking to a thoughtful parent.

### How it sounds when it's right
- Short sentences. Specific nouns. Concrete claims.
- Conversational without being casual.
- Respectful of the parent's intelligence.
- Calm, not pressed.
- Generous, not selling.

### How you know it's wrong (read-aloud questions)
- *Could TutorCo say this?* → Brand-flat. Rewrite until it's recognisably Cuemath.
- *Would a parent at the school gate roll their eyes?* → Brochure. Rewrite as conversation.
- *Do I hear two sentences with the same rhythm in a row?* → Parallel "we don't / we do" structure. Break it.
- *Does the closing line summarise the page back at me?* → Slogan. Cut it.
- *Did I just use a word that means nothing specific?* → "unlock potential," "bright future," "world-class," "transformative," "empower," "love for learning," "powerful." Cut and rewrite specific.
- *Is the headline true for any ed-tech?* → Generic. Rewrite for THIS cell, THIS week.

### Banned hero opener patterns (these almost always fail)
- "Today I want to talk about…"
- "Whether your child is K-12…"
- "At Cuemath, we believe…"
- "Here's the thing…"
- Section-header chyrons used as opening lines

## Premium check — the final gate

Before you mark an LP ready for review, walk it in the parent's shoes one last time:

1. Does the headline make me *want* to scroll, or does it make me *click back*?
2. By the time I reach the form, do I feel respected, or worked-on?
3. Is there a single sentence in here that a different ed-tech brand could plausibly say? If yes, that sentence is a vulnerability — fix it.
4. Does the page leave me with a feeling about Cuemath, or just facts?
5. If I forwarded this to a friend, what's the one sentence I'd quote to them? Is that sentence strong enough to forward?

## When to write three variants (the variant rule, restated)

Three variants is the default for *net-new* LPs. But variants are useful only when they *test a real hypothesis about the story*. Three pages with the same narrative spine, different word choices, are one page written three ways. That's not testing — that's polishing.

Variants should differ on the story:
- **Same story, different opening hook?** That's not a variant. That's a draft.
- **Different validation pattern (we see you vs we've helped families like you)?** Variant.
- **Different mechanism emphasis (tutor-continuity vs method vs outcomes)?** Variant.
- **Different proof slice (longitudinal-coach vs same-test-prep vs same-grade-band)?** Variant.

If you can't articulate the hypothesis each variant tests, don't ship three — ship one.

For *revisions of a live LP* (most LP work in practice), single iteration is the right mode. Audit the current page against the five-step think, fix what's broken, iterate fold-by-fold.

For *variant generation on a winner*, two variants (Stretch + Wild) testing new angles against the existing winner — skip Safe (the current LP is Safe).
